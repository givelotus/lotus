Executive Summary & Architectural Thesis

Traditional Proof-of-Work blockchains perform post-PoW block propagation and validation: a miner privately selects a mempool snapshot, constructs a block, finds a PoW nonce, and then broadcasts a compact block (cmpctblock or Graphene) across the peer-to-peer network. Even with compact blocks, receiving nodes must:

  1. Reconcile any missing transactions that arrived late or differed from the miner's mempool,
  2. Reconstruct the ordered transaction vector and recompute the block's transaction Merkle root (tx_root),
  3. Execute all state transitions (UTXO deletions and insertions) and recompute the post-block UTXO state commitment (state_root) on the critical path of block relay before forwarding or building on the new tip.

At high throughput (e.g., $10^5$–$10^6$ transactions per 120-second block), doing set reconciliation, Merklix tree updates, and libmdbx B+Tree page modifications after PoW is found creates a propagation and validation bottleneck that increases orphan rates and centralizes mining pressure.

Lotus V2's commitment architecture (P02) unlocks a qualitatively different paradigm—Progressive Bulk Template Pre-Consensus with Persistent Merklix Overlays:

  1. 100% Miner-Independent Subtree A and UtxoRoot:
  2. Non-coinbase transactions (vtx[1 .. n - 1]) are canonically ordered by TxID (CTOR) and committed in Subtree A, a 256-bit TxID-prefix-keyed single-leaf-collapsed Merklix tree whose root combines with the winning miner's CoinbaseLeaf in a single $O(1)$ top-level hash: $$\text{TxRoot} = H_{\text{txroot}}(\texttt{0x03} \parallel \text{CoinbaseLeaf} \parallel \text{SubtreeA})$$
  3. Under delayed/pipelined coinbase UTXO insertion, Block $h$'s coinbase outputs are inserted into UtxoRoot at the start of Block $h+1$ (when Block $h$ is already connected and its coinbase_txid is immutable), while Block $h$'s own candidate transactions (vtx[1 .. n - 1]) determine 100% of the UTXO mutations during Block $h$.
  4. Consequently, **every node on the network can pre-validate and pre-compute the exact post-block SubtreeA root, UtxoRoot, cumulative fees, and cumulative block cost units ($C_{\text{block}}$) before a PoW block is ever found**, without knowing which miner will win Block $h$ or what coinbase payout addresses/extranonce that miner will use.
  1. Solving the Three Hard Problems of Bulk Pre-Consensus:
  2. Problem 1 (Distributed Set Reconciliation): Why probabilistic whole-mempool schemes (Graphene's Bloom + IBLT and MinHash) fail catastrophically in adversarial P2P environments, and how Merklix Prefix-Count Bisection + Exact-Count-Guided Minisketch ($GF(2^{64})$ BCH syndromes) over Subtree A's native (count, root) internal nodes achieves zero-cliff, deterministic reconciliation.
  3. Problem 2 (Transaction Receipt Order & Defining the Reconciled Set): How continuous, uncoordinated P2P transaction arrival is discretized into Progressive Epoch Overlay Batches ($\Delta_1, \Delta_2, \dots, \Delta_k$) every $\tau_{\text{epoch}} \in [2\text{ s}, 5\text{ s}]$, binding TxID to exact witness TxHash variants and resolving double-spends and intra-template parent-child chains cleanly.
  4. Problem 3 (Progressively Overlayed Mempools in RAM + Zero-Copy libmdbx): How to store $k \approx 24$ progressive mempool overlay layers ($L_0 \to L_1 \to \dots \to L_k \to L_{\text{spec}}$) in a Multi-Layer Persistent (Copy-on-Write / Functional) Radix-Merklix Arena where each overlay step updates both Subtree A (TxRoot) and UtxoRoot in $O(|\Delta_i| \log N)$ time (single-digit milliseconds) and tens of megabytes of total RAM, reducing post-PoW block validation and libmdbx commit to an $O(1)$ microsecond operation.

Problem 1: Distributed Set Reconciliation (Why IBLT/Bloom Fails, and What Works)

1.1 Critique of Graphene (Bloom Filter + IBLT) and MinHash in UTXO Networks

Historically, bulk block/template compression proposals in Bitcoin and Bitcoin Cash centered on Graphene (combining a variable-sized Bloom filter with an Invertible Bloom Lookup Table, or IBLT) and MinHash sketches. Neither achieved reliable production deployment for pre-consensus or adversarial P2P relay, for fundamental information-theoretic and security reasons:

A. The IBLT Parameterization Cliff (Peeling Failure)

An Invertible Bloom Lookup Table (IBLT) encodes a set into $m = c \cdot d$ cells (typically $c \approx 1.3$–$1.5$, with $k \in \{3, 4\}$ hash functions), where $d = |A \triangle B| = |A \setminus B| + |B \setminus A|$ is the size of the symmetric difference between sender set $A$ and receiver set $B$.

  • Decoding an IBLT relies on iterative peeling of a random hypergraph: finding cells with count == ±1 (pure cells), removing their elements from the other $k-1$ cells they touch, and repeating until all cells are empty.
  • Phase-transition cliff: Random $k$-uniform hypergraph peeling exhibits a sharp threshold around $c_{\text{crit}}(k)$ (the 2-core emergence threshold). If the actual symmetric difference $d_{\text{actual}}$ exceeds the capacity $d_{\text{param}}$ for which the IBLT was sized by even 10–15%, the hypergraph traps a non-empty 2-core (a cycle of cells all having $|\text{count}| \ge 2$).
  • When peeling stalls on a 2-core, IBLT recovery drops to 0% of the remaining elements. Every single unresolved transaction in the 2-core is lost simultaneously, forcing a fallback round-trip that wastes the entire IBLT payload and adds a full RTT of latency on the critical path.

B. Failure of the Graphene Bloom Filter Estimator

To keep the IBLT small, Graphene first sends a Bloom filter $BF(A)$ of the block's transactions so the receiver can filter its mempool $M$ down to a candidate set $B \subseteq M$ and bound false positives $FP = |B \setminus A|$.

  • Sizing the IBLT still requires the sender to guess how many transactions in $A$ the receiver is missing ($FN = |A \setminus B|$). The sender does not know the receiver's mempool!
  • If the receiver recently connected, experienced a brief network stall, has slightly different fee/orphan policy, or hasn't received the latest 500 transactions, $FN = |A \setminus B|$ exceeds the sender's guess, and the IBLT immediately hits the 2-core peeling cliff.

C. Adversarial Boundary / Churn Injection Attacks (P2P DoS)

In a permissionless P2P network, the symmetric difference $d = |A \triangle B|$ is adversarially controllable:

  • An attacker can broadcast a burst of low-cost or replacement-boundary transactions to half the network right before an epoch boundary or block announcement, or selectively trickle distinct valid transactions to different peer partitions.
  • For a negligible fee budget (or zero fee if exploiting orphan/policy edges), the attacker inflates $|A \triangle B|$ across honest peers beyond $d_{\text{param}}$, causing 100% IBLT decode failure network-wide.
  • Furthermore, in an unkeyed or predictable IBLT/Bloom construction, an adversary can grind transaction hashes that collide into the same IBLT cells (a targeted 2-core poisoning attack), guaranteeing peeling failure even when $|A \triangle B| \ll d_{\text{param}}$.

D. Why MinHash Cannot Perform Set Reconciliation

MinHash (Minimum Hash sketching) maintains $k$ minimum hash values over a set $A$ to estimate the Jaccard similarity $J(A, B) = \frac{|A \cap B|}{|A \cup B|}$ with standard error $O(1/\sqrt{k})$.

  • MinHash is an estimator of set overlap cardinality, not an error-correcting code or set-difference recovery algorithm.
  • Knowing that $J(A, B) \approx 0.985 \pm 0.01$ over $100{,}000$ transactions tells a node that roughly $1{,}500$ transactions differ, but identifies zero of the specific missing TxHashes outside the $k$ sampled minima. It provides neither exact difference counts per prefix nor algebraic recovery of the missing elements.

1.2 Merklix Prefix-Count Bisection (Native to Subtree A)

In Lotus V2, Subtree A is already a 256-bit TxID-prefix-keyed Merklix radix tree (P02). Unlike Bitcoin's positional Merkle tree (where internal nodes commit only to positional slices and carry no key-range semantics), every internal node at depth $d$ (0 <= d < 256) of Subtree A covers a deterministic prefix $p \in \{0, 1\}^d$ of the 256-bit TxID space and commits to both the exact leaf count and the full 256-bit (32-byte) subtree cryptographic root of its left and right children:

TxLeaf(TxID, TxHash)                 = H_txroot(0x00 || TxID || TxHash)
TxMerklixNode(d, c_L, root_L, c_R, root_R) = H_txroot(0x01 || u16(d) || u32(c_L) || root_L || u32(c_R) || root_R)
EmptyTxMerklixRoot                   = H_txroot(0x02)

(Note: For a depth-$d$ subtree with count $c = c_L + c_R$, the canonical node summary is (count: u32, root: h32) = 36 bytes).

How Buckets Are Sized and Why Single-Leaf Collapse Never Breaks Cross-Node Bucket Correspondence

[!IMPORTANT] Maintainer Question & Mathematical Proof: "How are buckets sized? Is it based on total mempool transaction count? How do we know that buckets across nodes correspond to a particular Merklix subtree that's shared? If it's a collapsed tree, it seems like that could be a problem."

  1. Buckets Are Fixed $k$-Bit Key-Space Prefixes of TxID — NEVER Count-Based Array Slices:
  2. A bucket at depth $k$ is never sized or partitioned by dividing the node's total mempool count $N$ into equal-sized array chunks (which would shift boundaries whenever a single transaction is added or missing!).
  3. Instead, because Subtree A is a radix trie over the 256-bit TxID key space $[0, 2^{256}-1)$, a bucket at depth $k$ is defined strictly by the first $k$ bits of the transaction's 256-bit TxID: $$\text{Bucket}(k, p) \;=\; \left\{\, tx \;\middle|\; \text{first } k \text{ bits of } tx.\text{TxID} == p \,\right\}, \qquad p \in [0, 2^k - 1]$$
  4. For example, at depth $k = 8$ ($2^8 = 256$ buckets, 0x00..0xFF), bucket 0x4a is the set of all transactions whose TxID begins with byte 0x4a.
  5. Because TxID is a uniform 256-bit BLAKE3 hash:
  6. Deterministic Cross-Node Correspondence: A transaction with TxID = 0x4a... always belongs to bucket 0x4a at depth 8 (and bucket 0x4 at depth 4, bucket 0x4a3 at depth 12) on every node in the network, regardless of whether Node A has 10 transactions and Node B has 100,000 transactions!
  7. Statistical Balance Without Dynamic Re-Slicing: By the uniform hash property of BLAKE3, if a block/epoch contains $N$ transactions, each depth-$k$ prefix bucket naturally receives $\text{Binomial}(N, 2^{-k}) \approx N / 2^k$ transactions without ever moving bucket boundaries.
  1. Why Single-Leaf Collapse (src/lotus_v2/sparse_state.cpp:54–69) Never Breaks Depth-$k$ Subtree Sharing:
  2. A natural concern with a collapsed trie is: "What if Bucket 0x4a has only 1 transaction (so it collapses), or what if a sibling bucket 0x4b is empty on Node P (count = 0) so Bucket 0x4a's single leaf collapses upward to depth 7 on Node P, whereas on Node Q Bucket 0x4b is non-empty (count = 1) so an internal branch node exists at depth 7? Doesn't that change Bucket 0x4a's hash?"
  3. No! Look at the exact Merklix recursion in src/lotus_v2/sparse_state.cpp:54–69: ``cpp // Exact Lotus V2 Single-Leaf-Collapsed Merklix recursion (sparse_state.cpp:54-69): Hash RootOf(std::span<const LeafEntry> entries, uint16_t depth) { if (entries.empty()) return EmptyTxMerklixRoot(); // count == 0: 0x02 (depth-independent!) if (entries.size() == 1) return entries.front().leaf_hash; // count == 1: 0x00 || TxID || TxHash (depth-independent!) // count >= 2: split entries by bit depth into left (bit=0) and right (bit=1): return TxMerklixNode(depth, left.size(), RootOf(left, depth + 1), right.size(), RootOf(right, depth + 1)); // 0x01 || u16(depth) || ... } ``
  4. Notice two fundamental algebraic properties of RootOf(E_p, k) for any $k$-bit prefix bucket $E_p = \text{Bucket}(k, p)$:
  5. Locality Theorem (RootOf(E_p, k) Depends ONLY on $E_p$):
  6. If $|E_p| = 0$: (count[p], root[p]) = (0, EmptyTxMerklixRoot) (H_txroot(0x02)).
  7. If $|E_p| = 1$: (count[p], root[p]) = (1, TxLeaf(tx)) (H_txroot(0x00 || TxID || TxHash) — notice TxLeaf does NOT hash depth!).
  8. If $|E_p| \ge 2$: (count[p], root[p]) = (|E_p|, TxMerklixNode(k, |E_{p||0}|, RootOf(E_{p||0}, k+1), |E_{p||1}|, RootOf(E_{p||1}, k+1))) (which hashes internal branches strictly at depths $\ge k$).
  9. In all three cases (count == 0, count == 1, count >= 2), (count[p], root[p]) at depth $k$ is a pure function of $(k, E_p)$ and is 100% independent of every other bucket $p' \ne p$ in the tree!
  10. Exact Upward Rollup Theorem (CombineSubtrees from Depth $k \to 0$):
  11. How do the $2^k$ bucket summaries (count[p], root[p]) at depth $k$ roll up to the global SubtreeA_Root = RootOf(AllTxs, 0) even when some buckets have count == 0 or count == 1?
  12. At each parent level $d = k-1$ down to $0$, combining left child (c_L, root_L) and right child (c_R, root_R) applies the exact same single-leaf collapse rule: $$\text{CombineSubtrees}(d, (c_L, r_L), (c_R, r_R)) = \begin{cases} (0,\; \text{EmptyTxMerklixRoot}) & \text{if } c_L + c_R = 0 \\ (1,\; r_L) & \text{if } c_L = 1 \text{ and } c_R = 0 \;\text{(singleton collapses upward untouched!)} \\ (1,\; r_R) & \text{if } c_L = 0 \text{ and } c_R = 1 \;\text{(singleton collapses upward untouched!)} \\ (c_L + c_R,\; \text{TxMerklixNode}(d, c_L, r_L, c_R, r_R)) & \text{if } c_L + c_R \ge 2 \end{cases}$$
  13. Because a singleton leaf's hash TxLeaf(tx) does not depend on depth, collapsing (1, TxLeaf(tx)) upward from depth $k$ to depth $d < k$ when sibling buckets are empty produces the bit-for-bit identical 32-byte hash as evaluating RootOf(AllTxs, 0) from the top!
  14. Conclusion: Any two nodes that have the same transactions starting with $k$-bit prefix p are mathematically guaranteed to have the exact same (count[p], root[p]) at depth $k$, regardless of single-leaf collapsing or what exists in any other bucket $p' \ne p$.

How Depth-$k$ Merklix Prefix Exchange Isolates Differences

Suppose Node $P$ and Node $Q$ hold candidate transaction sets $A$ and $B$ (or epoch batch sets) of size $N \approx 100{,}000$, and their top-level (count, SubtreeA_Root) differs.

  1. Full 256-Bit (32-Byte) Merklix Roots on Sparse Dirty Subtrees (Zero 64-Bit Truncation):
  2. At depth $k$ (e.g., $k = 8 \implies 256$ shards, or adaptive $k \in [2..8]$ per Section 1.5), Subtree A partitions the 256-bit TxID space into $S = 2^k$ disjoint prefix buckets $s \in [0, 2^k - 1]$.
  3. For each prefix bucket $s$, a node already has the cached Merklix summary (count[s]: u32, root[s]: h32) in RAM (maintained natively by its $2^k$ horizontal shards or Merklix overlay).
  4. Why Full 32-Byte Roots Are Mandatory (Preventing $2^{32}$ Birthday Root Forgery): Truncating bucket roots to 64 bits (root64) would allow an adversary to grind two distinct transaction subsets in a bucket that collide on a 64-bit truncated root in $2^{32} \approx 4.3 \times 10^9$ BLAKE3 evaluations (< 1 second on a single GPU!), causing honest nodes to think a bucket matches when their true 256-bit SubtreeA roots diverge. Because Adaptive Top-Down Polling (Section 1.5) only transmits dirty/mismatched subtrees, every bucket comparison uses the full 256-bit (32-byte) root[s]: h32 ($128\text{-bit}$ birthday security).
  5. Immediate Isolation of Identical Shards:
  6. For every shard $s \in [0, 2^k - 1]$ where count_P[s] == count_Q[s] and root_P[s] == root_Q[s] (full 32-byte equality), the entire subtree of $N / 2^k$ transactions is 100% cryptographically identical (including both TxID and TxHash at every leaf!). Zero further work is performed on matching shards.
  7. Exact Count Delta in Differing Shards:
  8. For any shard $s$ where root_P[s] != root_Q[s], both nodes immediately learn the net cardinality difference $\Delta c_s = |\text{count}_P[s] - \text{count}_Q[s]|$ and the total subtree size $c_s = \max(\text{count}_P[s], \text{count}_Q[s])$.
  9. Pure Bisection Trade-Off (RTTs vs. Bandwidth):
  10. If reconciliation used pure interactive bisection down the Merklix tree (stepping 4 bits / 16-way per round-trip from depth 8 to depth 16):
  11. Bandwidth is minimal when $d = |A \triangle B|$ is tiny ($d \le 20$), requiring only $O(d \log(N/d))$ node summaries.
  12. Latency (RTTs) scales as $O(\log_{2^b}(N/d))$ round-trips (typically 2 to 4 RTTs to reach singleton leaves). Across intercontinental P2P links ($100\text{–}150\text{ ms}$ RTT), 3–4 interactive RTTs cost $300\text{–}600\text{ ms}$.
  13. Can we get the 1-RTT latency of algebraic sketching and the exact shard isolation and zero-cliff determinism of Merklix trees? Yes—by combining Merklix prefix counts with Minisketch.

1.3 Hybrid Merklix Prefix Buckets + Exact-Count-Guided Minisketch ($GF(2^{64})$ BCH Syndromes)

Unlike IBLT (which uses random hash peeling and fails on 2-core cycles), Minisketch (PinSketch / BIP330 Erlay) operates algebraically over the finite field $GF(2^{64})$:

  • Each element $x \in GF(2^{64}) \setminus \{0\}$ is a 64-bit short transaction identifier: $$x = \text{SipHash-2-4}(k_0^{(e)}, k_1^{(e)}, \text{TxHash})$$ (if the 64-bit SipHash output is 0, it is mapped to 1 so $x \in GF(2^{64})^\times$).
  • Unpredictable Per-Epoch SipHash Key Derivation: To prevent an adversary from pre-grinding transactions whose 64-bit short_ids collide ($x(T_1) = x(T_2) \implies x_1 \oplus x_2 = 0$) or form a forged 3-tuple $(x_1, x_2, x_3)$ in $GF(2^{64})$ before an epoch begins, the 128-bit SipHash key $(k_0^{(e)}, k_1^{(e)})$ is rotated on every epoch $e$ using the unpredictability of the parent PoW block and the preceding finalized overlay: $$(k_0^{(e)}, k_1^{(e)}) = \text{BLAKE3\_Keyed\_128}\!\left(\texttt{"Lotus/v2/preconsensus/sipkey"},\; B_h.\text{BlockID} \parallel \text{u32}(e) \parallel L_{e-1}.\text{overlay\_id}\right)$$
  • A sketch of capacity $t$ for a set $S$ consists of the first $t$ odd power-sum syndromes in $GF(2^{64})$: $$\sigma_j(S) = \sum_{x \in S} x^{2j - 1} \pmod{P_{64}(X)} \quad \text{for } j = 1, 2, \dots, t$$ where addition in $GF(2^{64})$ is bitwise XOR ($\oplus$).
  • Because XOR is linear over $GF(2^{64})$, combining two sketches $\Sigma(A) \oplus \Sigma(B)$ yields the exact power-sum syndromes of their symmetric difference $\Sigma(A \triangle B)$: $$\sigma_j(A) \oplus \sigma_j(B) = \sum_{x \in A \triangle B} x^{2j - 1}$$
  • Zero Peeling Cliff (Deterministic Berlekamp–Massey + Chien/Cantor–Zassenhaus Root Finding):
  • If $|A \triangle B| \le t$, Berlekamp–Massey finds the unique degree-$d$ error-locator polynomial $\Lambda(z) = \prod_{x \in A \triangle B} (1 - x z)$ and factors it over $GF(2^{64})$ with 100% algebraic certainty (save for a $2^{-64}$ field collision probability) and 8 bytes per difference element ($t \times 8\text{ bytes}$ total, vs. IBLT's ~18–24 bytes per element with a peeling cliff).

Inline 16-Byte Bucket Syndromes (s1, s3) for $O(1)$ 1-or-2-Tx Recovery (Zero Bloom / Zero IBLT)

Even Minisketch requires choosing a capacity $t \ge |A \triangle B|$ when applied monolithically to a whole block. Combining Subtree A's depth-$k$ Merklix prefix buckets with fast epochs ($\tau = 0.5\text{s}\text{–}2.0\text{s}$) and fixed 2-element $GF(2^{64})$ bucket syndromes (s1, s3) eliminates dynamic sketch sizing, Bloom filters, and IBLTs entirely:

  • At depth $k = 8$ ($256$ buckets) with a $\tau = 0.5\text{s}\text{–}2.0\text{s}$ epoch cadence, even at $1{,}000\text{ tx/s}$, each bucket receives an average of only $2\text{ to }8$ new transactions per epoch, and after the Stage 2 Maturation Barrier (Section 2.2), boundary arrival jitter affects at most $|\Delta_s| = 1\text{ or }2$ transactions in the few buckets that differ!
  • Therefore, every Merklix bucket summary at depth $k$ maintains two running 64-bit $GF(2^{64})$ power-sum syndromes over the transactions in bucket $s$: $$s_1[s] = \bigoplus_{x \in \text{Bucket}_s} x, \qquad s_3[s] = \bigoplus_{x \in \text{Bucket}_s} x^3 \pmod{P_{64}(X)}$$
  • Maintaining (s1, s3) costs two 64-bit XORs (~2 nanoseconds) whenever a transaction is inserted into or removed from a bucket!
  • When two nodes compare bucket $s$ and see root_P[s] != root_Q[s], they compute: $$\Delta s_1 = s_{1, P}[s] \oplus s_{1, Q}[s], \qquad \Delta s_3 = s_{3, P}[s] \oplus s_{3, Q}[s], \qquad \Delta c = |\text{count}_P[s] - \text{count}_Q[s]|$$
  • 1-Tx Difference ($|\Delta_s| = 1$, i.e., $\Delta c = 1$ and $(\Delta s_1)^3 \equiv \Delta s_3 \pmod{P_{64}}$):
  • $\Delta s_1$ yields the candidate 64-bit short_id of the single differing transaction in 1 CPU instruction.
  • 2-Tx Difference ($|\Delta_s| = 2$, i.e., $\Delta c \in \{0, 2\}$ and quadratic $z^2 + (\Delta s_1) z + \frac{\Delta s_3 \oplus (\Delta s_1)^3}{\Delta s_1} = 0$ has two distinct roots in $GF(2^{64})$):
  • Solves the degree-2 quadratic over $GF(2^{64})$ in ~80 nanoseconds to recover both candidate 64-bit short_ids $x_1, x_2$.
  • Rare $\ge 3$-Tx Difference in Bucket $s$ (or Failed Root Verification):
  • Simply bisect 1 or 2 levels deeper in the Merklix tree for only that specific bucket $s$ (where its 4 children at depth $k+2$ each have $1/4$ the transactions, reducing child differences back to $\le 2$).

[!CAUTION] Normative Security Invariant — The Strict 256-Bit Root-Verification Gate: Any 64-bit short_id $x$ (or pair $x_1, x_2$) algebraically decoded from (Δs1, Δs3) is treated strictly as an untrusted lookup hint into the local MempoolTxStore (or peer getdata). A node MUST NEVER mark a bucket reconciled or vote YES on a peer's bucket root root_Q[s]: h32 merely because (Δs1, Δs3) yielded a valid polynomial root in $GF(2^{64})$. Instead, the node MUST:

  1. Resolve the full transaction(s) matching $x$, verify their consensus validity, and tentatively apply them to Subtree A bucket $s$,
  2. Recompute the full 256-bit (32-byte) Merklix root recomputed_root[s]: h32, and
  3. Verify exact 32-byte equality recomputed_root[s] == root_Q[s].

If recomputed_root[s] != root_Q[s] (e.g., due to a $2^{-64}$ syndrome collision or a Byzantine peer spoofing syndromes or hiding $\ge 3$ differences that alias a degree-1/degree-2 equation), the syndrome decode is immediately discarded and the node falls back to deterministic 256-bit Merklix child bisection on bucket $s$.

2.1 Why Naive Monolithic Mempool Hashing and "Every 100 Txns" Triggers Fail

A. Why Hashing the Monolithic Mempool Fails

Suppose $M = 1{,}000$ honest nodes are connected over a global P2P network with one-way propagation delay $\tau_{\text{prop}} \approx 100\text{ ms}$ and transaction arrival rate $\lambda = 1{,}000\text{ tx/s}$.

  • Even if two nodes $P$ and $Q$ agree on 99.9% of their mempool transactions and differ on just 1 single in-flight boundary transaction, cryptographic hash functions (BLAKE3) have the avalanche property: changing a single leaf changes 100% of the bits of a monolithic SubtreeA_Root and UtxoRoot!
  • With $b = 20$ boundary transactions in flight across the network, there are up to $2^{20} \approx 10^6$ possible subsets of boundary transactions. Every node computes a completely distinct monolithic root hash, and Avalanche can never form a quorum.

B. Why "Every 100 Transactions" Cannot Be Used to Trigger an Epoch Boundary

In an uncoordinated, leaderless P2P network, count-triggered epochs fail for three structural reasons:

  1. Out-of-Order Arrival Desynchronizes the Cut: Because transactions arrive in different orders at different nodes, Node A's "first 100 transactions" ($\{T_1 \dots T_{98}, T_X, T_Y\}$) and Node B's "first 100 transactions" ($\{T_1 \dots T_{98}, T_Z, T_W\}$) are different sets even when both nodes have received 102 transactions total!
  2. Low-Traffic Liveness Stall: If network traffic drops to $2\text{ tx/s}$ (or $1$ transaction during a quiet period), waiting for $100$ transactions would stall pre-consensus finality for $50+$ seconds (or forever for a single transaction), defeating fast pre-consensus.
  3. Burst Epoch-Number Desynchronization: During a burst of $5{,}000\text{ tx/s}$, one side of the network would race to Epoch $e = 50$ in one second while another side is still polling on Epoch $e = 12$.

2.2 The Pipelined Avalanche Logical Clock (Ingest -> Mature -> Vote) & Local Receive-Epoch Stamping

What Exists Today vs. What We Add

In the current Lotus V2 codebase (src/lotus_v2/avalanche_poll.h), lotus::v2::ava::OutgoingPoll carries only a local per-peer request nonce (uint64_t round{}):

There is no logical or causal clock shared across peers in OutgoingPoll today.

To trigger epochs leaderlessly without wall-clock guessing or count desynchronization, we introduce the Piggybacked Avalanche Logical Epoch Clock ($e$) and Local Receive-Epoch Cohort Stamping:

  1. Piggybacked Lamport Logical Clock (finalized_epoch e):
  2. Every confirmed block height $h$ resets the logical epoch clock to $e = 0$ (L_0 = Base Block B_h + Pipelined Coinbase).
  3. Every avapoll and avaresponse message piggybacks a compact causal header: ``text CausalEpochHeader = tip_block_id:h32 || finalized_epoch:u32 || finalized_overlay_id:h32 ``
  4. This forms a strict Lamport causal chain $L_0 \prec L_1 \prec L_2 \prec \dots \prec L_e$: whenever a node reaches Avalanche finality on $L_e$ (or receives an avaresponse + verifies the 32-byte roots of $L_e$), it immediately advances its local logical clock $e_{\text{local}}$ to $e + 1$.
  1. Zero Wire-Cohort Trust (Local Receive-Epoch Stamping Prevents Backdated/Future-Dated Tag Spoofing):
  2. Why Sender-Provided Cohort Tags Are Forbidden: If P2P inv/tx relay carried a sender-asserted (cohort = e, Tx) tag, a Byzantine node could wait until the very last millisecond of Stage 2 (Epoch $e+1$) and broadcast 5,000 brand-new transactions carrying a backdated tag cohort = e to half the network right as Epoch $e+2$ begins voting, shattering candidate set convergence (or broadcast a future-dated tag cohort = e + 50 to poison future cohorts).
  3. Normative Local Stamping Rule: Standard P2P inv and tx messages carry zero cohort field on the wire. Instead, when any node receives and consensus-validates a transaction $T$ into its local MempoolTxStore, it stamps $T$ with its own local logical clock local_cohort(T) = e_local at the instant of local validation!
  1. The 3-Stage Hardware-Style Causal Pipeline (Ingest -> Mature -> Vote): Because honest P2P broadcast takes $\tau_{\text{prop}} \approx 100\text{–}150\text{ ms}$ while each Avalanche epoch takes $\tau_{\text{epoch}} \approx 500\text{–}1{,}000\text{ ms}$, local receive-epoch stamping + a 1-epoch maturation barrier guarantees that 95%+ of honestly broadcast transactions receive the exact same local_cohort = e across the network, and the remaining ~5% of epoch-boundary transactions have already arrived into every node's local mempool (local_cohort = e+1) a full epoch before voting on Cohort $e$ begins!

2.3 Intra-Epoch Bucket Convergence Vector $\mathbf{B}_e$ vs. Global Atomic Epoch Commit $L_e$

[!CAUTION] Critical Architectural Distinction — Why Subtree A Shards Reconcile Independently Within an Epoch, While UtxoRoot Commits Atomically at the Epoch Barrier: In Subtree A, transactions are keyed by their own TxID, and under OutpointKey = txid || vout, all created outputs of a transaction with prefix $s \in [0..255]$ also reside in UtxoRoot shard $s$. However, a transaction's spent inputs (prev_txid || prev_vout) spend UTXOs created by arbitrary historical transactions whose prev_txid prefixes $s' \ne s$ are uniformly distributed across all 256 UtxoRoot shards! Therefore:

  1. If Bucket $s_1$ depended on an unfinalized parent transaction in Bucket $s_2$ inside the same asynchronously advancing vector clock, or if UtxoRoot shards advanced at mismatched epoch numbers, cross-shard input deletions would create dangling UTXO references and Combinatorial State Root Explosion.
  2. To achieve both independent per-bucket reconciliation (so a contended bucket never stalls uncontended buckets) and a single canonical global UtxoRoot per epoch, Lotus V2 separates the Intra-Epoch Bucket Convergence Vector $\mathbf{B}_e$ from the Global Epoch Overlay Commit $L_e$:
  1. Cross-Bucket Dependency Invariant (Cohort Eligibility Rule):
  2. A transaction $T$ is eligible for inclusion in Cohort $c = e$ if and only if every outpoint $o = (\text{prev\_txid}, \text{prev\_vout})$ spent by $T$ already exists as an unspent coin in the previously finalized global overlay $L_e$ (or Base $L_0$)!
  3. What about an unconfirmed child transaction $T_{\text{child}}$ that spends an output of an unconfirmed parent $T_{\text{parent}}$?
  4. $T_{\text{parent}}$ is finalized in Epoch Overlay $L_e$ (~$0.5\text{–}1.0\text{ s}$).
  5. Immediately upon $L_e$'s commit, $T_{\text{parent}}$'s outputs exist in $L_e.\text{utxo\_root}$, so $T_{\text{child}}$ is automatically eligible for the very next cohort $c = e+1$ (~$0.5\text{–}1.0\text{ s}$ later)!
  6. (Note: Tier 0/1/2 full block validation in Section 2.6.A still supports arbitrary same-block parent-child chains; the Cross-Bucket Dependency Invariant applies strictly to candidate selection during pre-consensus Cohort $e$ so that the 256 Subtree A buckets have zero cross-bucket causal dependencies within Epoch $e$!)
  1. Intra-Epoch Bucket Convergence Vector $\mathbf{B}_e = \langle b_0, b_1, \dots, b_{255} \rangle$ (Parallel Subtree A Reconciliation):
  2. During Epoch $e+2$ (voting on Cohort $c = e$), if the top-level OverlayID does not finalize on Round 1 (Rule 3 of Section 1.5), the 256 prefix buckets of Subtree A reconcile in parallel.
  3. Each bucket $s \in [0..255]$ tracks its own convergence state $b_s \in \{\texttt{CLEAN}, \texttt{POLLING}, \texttt{LOCKED}(R^*_s)\}$:
  4. Uncontended buckets lock their winning 32-byte Subtree A bucket root $R^*_s$ in 1–2 rounds and stop polling (b_s = LOCKED).
  5. Any bucket $s$ that fails to lock within kMaxBucketPollRounds = 8 (~$400\text{ ms}$) simply keeps its previous epoch's bucket root $R^*_s = L_{e-1}.\text{shard\_tx\_roots}[s]$ (deferring its new transactions to Cohort $e+1$) so a single contended bucket can never stall epoch progression!
  1. Global Atomic Epoch Barrier ($L_{e-1} \to L_e$ Across All 256 UtxoRoot Shards):
  2. The instant all dirty buckets in $\mathbf{B}_e$ are LOCKED (or hit the 8-round deferral cap), the epoch batch $\Delta_e = \bigcup_{s=0}^{255} \Delta_{e, s}$ is completely determined.
  3. The node then executes a single atomic barrier transition $L_{e-1} \to L_e$:
  4. All spent input deletions $(\text{prev\_txid}, \text{prev\_vout})$ from $\Delta_e$ are routed by prev_txid[0..7] to their target UtxoRoot shards $s' \in [0..255]$.
  5. All created output insertions $(\text{txid}, \text{vout})$ from $\Delta_e$ are routed by txid[0..7] to their target UtxoRoot shards $s \in [0..255]$.
  6. All 256 UtxoRoot shards apply their sorted batch updates in parallel (~$1.35\text{ ms}$ across 8 threads) to produce the single deterministic global L_e.utxo_root, L_e.subtree_a_root, and L_e.overlay_id: $$\text{OverlayID}(L_e) = H_{\text{blockid}}(\texttt{"Lotus/v2/preconsensus/overlay\0"} \parallel B_h.\text{BlockID} \parallel \text{u32}(e) \parallel L_e.\text{subtree\_a\_root} \parallel L_e.\text{utxo\_root})$$

Hardening Superset Tie-Breaking Against the "Drip-Feed Superset Stall" Attack

Suppose an adversary attempts to prevent bucket $s$ from ever converging during Epoch $e+2$ by drip-feeding 1 brand-new valid transaction $T_{\text{drip}, r}$ every poll round $r = 1, 2, 3, \dots$ and advertising a successively larger candidate set $R_{\text{adv}}^{(r)}$ with count = k + r. If honest nodes naively switched to any higher-count valid superset on every round, the adversary could reset Avalanche confidence counters indefinitely for the price of 1 transaction fee per round!

To make Superset Tie-Breaking 100% immune to drip-feed liveness stalls, nodes enforce two strict gates:

  1. Gate 1 — Pre-Cutoff Local Eligibility Gate (validated_at_epoch <= e + 1):
  2. During Epoch $e+2$ (which votes on Cohort $c = e$), a node will ONLY consider promoting a missing transaction $T_x$ into a peer's higher-count superset candidate $R_A$ if $T_x$ was already validated in the node's own local MempoolTxStore prior to the start of Epoch $e+2$ (local_cohort(T_x) <= e + 1)!
  3. Any transaction that arrives over P2P after Epoch $e+2$ voting has begun has local_cohort >= e + 2 and is strictly ineligible for promotion into Epoch $e+2$. Thus, an attacker drip-feeding new transactions during voting cannot trigger a single superset switch on any honest node!
  4. Gate 2 — Confidence Lock-In Ceiling (kSupersetLockConfidence = 3):
  5. Superset preference switching (count_peer > count_self with $|\Delta_s| \le 2$ pre-cutoff eligible transactions verified against root_peer[s]: h32) is permitted only during the initial convergence phase while the node's current candidate confidence is < kSupersetLockConfidence ($3$ consecutive positive polls).
  6. Once any candidate root $R_s$ accumulates $\ge 3$ consecutive matching polls, the node locks its preference on $R_s$ (unless overturned by an explicit majority quorum vote) and refuses any further count-based superset jumps for the remainder of that epoch, deferring any straggler to $L_{e+1}$.

2.5 Binding TxID to TxHash (Preventing Witness / Argument Malleability Split)

In Lotus V2 (P01, N03):

  • TxID commits to chain_id || header_core || inputs_core_root || outputs_root (excluding input witness scripts, arguments, and signatures). TxID determines outpoints (txid || vout), conflict detection, and the 256-bit key path in Subtree A.
  • TxHash commits to chain_id || header_core || full_inputs_root || outputs_root (including all witness arguments and signatures).
  • In Subtree A, the key path is TxID, while the leaf hash at that path commits to both TxID and TxHash: $$\text{TxLeaf}(\text{TxID}, \text{TxHash}) = H_{\text{txroot}}(\texttt{0x00} \parallel \text{TxID} \parallel \text{TxHash})$$

The Malleability Hazard in Set Reconciliation: Suppose a transaction has two valid witness variants $(T_1, T_2)$ with the same TxID but different TxHashes ($\text{TxHash}_1 \ne \text{TxHash}_2$, e.g., different third-party signature padding or alternative MAST branch satisfaction).

  • If set reconciliation only exchanged TxIDs (or short IDs derived from TxID), Node $P$ (holding $T_1$) and Node $Q$ (holding $T_2$) would believe they have the same transaction set, yet they would compute different TxLeaf hashes and therefore different SubtreeA roots, breaking block validation!

Normative Protocol Rule for Variant Binding:

  1. Merklix Shard Roots Automatically Detect Variant Mismatch: Because TxLeaf hashes TxID || TxHash, if Node $P$ has (TxID, TxHash_1) and Node $Q$ has (TxID, TxHash_2) in shard $s$, their shard counts match (count_P[s] == count_Q[s]), but their 32-byte Merklix shard roots differ (root_P[s] != root_Q[s]).
  2. Syndromes (s1, s3) and Short-IDs MUST Key on TxHash (Never TxID):
  3. Every 64-bit $GF(2^{64})$ syndrome element $x \in GF(2^{64})$ and every 48-bit compact short ID is computed over the full TxHash: $$x = \text{SipHash-2-4}(k_0^{(e)}, k_1^{(e)}, \text{TxHash})$$
  4. If Node $P$ holds $T_1$ ($\text{TxHash}_1$) and Node $Q$ holds $T_2$ ($\text{TxHash}_2$) for the same TxID, the $GF(2^{64})$ syndrome difference $A_s \triangle B_s$ contains both $x(\text{TxHash}_1)$ and $x(\text{TxHash}_2)$ (a two-sided difference of size 2 in bucket $s$, decoded immediately by the degree-2 quadratic over (Δs1, Δs3) and verified via the 32-byte root[s] gate!).
  5. Upon decoding $x(\text{TxHash}_1)$, Node $Q$ fetches $T_1$ by TxHash_1 via getdata(MSG_TX, TxHash_1), verifies $T_1$, and binds $T_1$ as the canonical representative for that TxID in overlay $L_e$ (retaining $T_2$ in its bounded recent-variant cache per N03).

2.6 Intra-Template Parent-Child Spends & Seniority-Hardened Conflict Resolution

A. Intra-Template Parent-Child Spends Under CTOR (TxID Ordering)

In Lotus V2, non-coinbase transactions in a block (vtx[1 .. n - 1]) are canonically ordered by ascending 256-bit little-endian TxID (CTOR), and committed in Subtree A by TxID bit-path.

  • Because TxID is a cryptographic hash, a child transaction $T_{\text{child}}$ spending an output (T_parent.TxID, vout) has an ~50% probability that $T_{\text{child}}.\text{TxID} < T_{\text{parent}}.\text{TxID}$ in CTOR wire order!
  • In pre-consensus overlays, per the Cross-Bucket Dependency Invariant (Section 2.3), $T_{\text{parent}}$ is finalized in Layer $L_e$ ($\Delta_e$) and $T_{\text{child}}$ is finalized in Layer $L_{e+1}$ ($\Delta_{e+1}$, ~$0.5\text{–}1.0\text{ s}$ later). And in Tier 0 / Tier 1 block validation (or within $L_{\text{spec}}$), $T_{\text{parent}}$ and $T_{\text{child}}$ may also be applied together in the same batch.

How Validation and UtxoRoot Updates Handle Intra-Template Spends Commutatively:

  1. Across Overlay Layers ($T_{\text{parent}} \in \Delta_e, T_{\text{child}} \in \Delta_{e+1}$ — Standard Pre-Consensus Path):
  2. When $\Delta_e$ is applied to $L_{e-1} \to L_e$, (T_parent.TxID, vout) is inserted into utxo_trie_root_e (UtxoRoot of $L_e$) and T_parent is inserted into tx_trie_root_e (Subtree A of $L_e$).
  3. When $\Delta_{e+1}$ is applied to $L_e \to L_{e+1}$:
  4. In Subtree A, T_child is inserted at its 256-bit TxID path into tx_trie_root_e to produce tx_trie_root_{e+1} (whether T_child.TxID is less than or greater than T_parent.TxID does not matter—Subtree A is a key-addressed radix trie!).
  5. In UtxoRoot, (T_parent.TxID, vout) already exists as a live leaf in utxo_trie_root_e! Applying T_child simply deletes (T_parent.TxID, vout) from utxo_trie_root_e and inserts T_child's spendable outputs to produce utxo_trie_root_{e+1}.
  6. Within the Same Batch ($T_{\text{parent}}, T_{\text{child}} \in \Delta_{\text{block}}$ — Tier 0/1 Block Validation or $L_{\text{spec}}$):
  7. Within a single block/tail batch $\Delta_{\text{block}}$, UTXO state transitions are a pure commutative map mutation on outpoints:
  8. Let $C(\Delta_{\text{block}})$ be the set of all spendable coins created by transactions in $\Delta_{\text{block}}$, and $S(\Delta_{\text{block}})$ be the set of all outpoints spent by transactions in $\Delta_{\text{block}}$.
  9. Every input in $S(\Delta_{\text{block}})$ is resolved either from the base overlay's UTXO state or from $C(\Delta_{\text{block}})$ (checking that no cycle exists—which is cryptographically impossible without breaking BLAKE3 TxID preimage resistance—and that timelock/sequence rules pass; note that intra-block spends require relative locktime 0).
  10. Any ephemeral outpoint created and spent within the same batch ($o \in C(\Delta_{\text{block}}) \cap S(\Delta_{\text{block}})$) is validated in RAM (feeding its Coin record directly to the child's VM execution and owninput/sighash checks) and elided from the UtxoRoot trie update altogether (net insertion + deletion = no-op on utxo_trie_root!).

B. Double-Spend Conflict Resolution with Cohort Seniority (Defeating Late Conflict Censorship)

When two conflicting transactions $T_A$ and $T_B$ ($\text{TxID}_A \ne \text{TxID}_B$) spend the same outpoint $o = (\text{prev\_txid}, \text{prev\_vout})$:

  1. Single-Owner Shard Routing: Because outpoints are keyed by OutpointKey = prev_txid || prev_vout (P02), the UTXO shard owning prev_txid's $k$-bit prefix is the single authoritative conflict detector for outpoint $o$ on every node.
  2. Cohort Seniority / First-Seen Maturation Lock (Preventing Zero-Cost Censorship Griefing):
  3. The Griefing Hazard: If any conflicting transaction $T_B$ arriving during Stage 2 (Maturation, Epoch $e+1$) or Stage 3 (Voting, Epoch $e+2$) could force a matured Cohort $e$ transaction $T_A$ into a withheld ConflictSet[o], an attacker who sees a victim's transaction $T_A$ in Stage 1 could wait until Stage 2, broadcast a conflicting double-spend $T_B$, and force honest nodes to withhold $T_A$ from $\Delta_e$ for zero fee cost (since neither $T_A$ nor $T_B$ would be included!).
  4. Normative Cohort Seniority Rule: Once Cohort $c = e$ closes at the end of Stage 1 (L_e finalizes and local clock advances to $e_{\text{local}} = e + 1$), any transaction $T_A$ already admitted into Cohort $c = e$ acquires Cohort Seniority over outpoint $o$. A conflicting transaction $T_B$ that arrives in a strictly later cohort (local_cohort(T_B) >= e + 1) MUST NOT displace $T_A$ or trigger ConflictSet[o] withholding for Cohort $e$; instead, $T_B$ is held as a subordinate standby candidate that is discarded as soon as $T_A$ finalizes in $L_e$.
  5. Same-Cohort Contention (local_cohort(T_A) == local_cohort(T_B) == e):
  6. Only when two conflicting transactions $T_A$ and $T_B$ arrive within the same Stage 1 cohort window $e$ do nodes place {TxID_A, TxID_B} into ConflictSet[o] and withhold outpoint $o$ from Cohort $e$ while 1 epoch of per-conflict Avalanche polling resolves the winning TxID for Cohort $e+1$.
  7. Immutability Once Locked in $L_e$: Once $T_A$ is finalized into an overlay layer $L_e$, outpoint $o$ is marked spent in utxo_trie_root_e. Any subsequently arriving conflicting transaction $T_B$ is immediately rejected against $L_e$.

2.7 Preventing Overlapping Epoch Reconciliation & Single-Step Cumulative Fast-Forward (L_{N-1} -> L_M)

[!IMPORTANT] Maintainer Question & Failure Mode Analysis: "Also, I have concerns about overlapping reconciliation for an epoch. Let's say epoch $N$ takes 10 seconds to reconcile due to whatever reason, and epoch $N+1$ is somehow started, what happens?"

If a node ever tried to reconcile Epoch $N$ and Epoch $N+1$ concurrently, three things would immediately break:

  1. Undefined Parent State: Epoch $N+1$'s candidate transactions depend on which UTXOs were created or spent in $L_N$, and Epoch $N+1$'s P05 parabolic fee-burn floor $\Delta\text{Burn}(E_n, C_{N}, C_{N} + \Delta C_{N+1})$ depends on $L_N$'s cumulative block cost $C_N$.
  2. Undefined SipHash Key: Epoch $N+1$'s syndrome key $(k_0^{(N+1)}, k_1^{(N+1)})$ is cryptographically derived from L_N.overlay_id.
  3. Bandwidth & Waiter Multiplication: Concurrent GETSHORTTX / GETMERKLIXBUCK queues for overlapping epochs would fetch the same missing transactions twice and corrupt PendingCandidateTracker.

Lotus V2 eliminates overlapping epoch reconciliation entirely through two orthogonal guarantees—one for the network-wide quorum and one for a locally lagging node:

Guarantee 1: Network-Wide Strict Single-Flight Epoch Invariant (Completion-Clocked, Never Timer-Clocked)

  1. No Wall-Clock Timer Triggers Epoch $N+1$:
  2. Epochs are never started by a periodic wall-clock timer (e.g., "start a new epoch every 1 second"). The ~0.5–1.0s epoch cadence is an emergent property of completion, not a clock trigger.
  3. Epoch $N+1$ is causally defined on top of $L_N$: its CausalEpochHeader commits to parent_overlay_id = L_N.overlay_id, and its SipHash key is derived from L_N.overlay_id. Therefore, it is cryptographically and structurally impossible for the network to start polling or reconciling Epoch $N+1$ before $L_N$ has finalized.
  4. While Epoch $N$ is still reconciling/polling, newly arriving P2P transactions simply continue accumulating in the open Stage-1 Ingest buffer (MempoolTxStore).
  5. Hard Per-Bucket Poll Cap (kMaxBucketPollRounds = 6, $\le 1.5\text{ s}$ Ceiling) Prevents Network-Wide 10-Second Stalls:
  6. Why would Epoch $N$ ever try to take 10 seconds across the network? Only if a dirty bucket $s \in [0..255]$ suffered sustained contention or slow GETSHORTTX fetches across many peers.
  7. By the Bounded Per-Bucket Poll Cap (Section 2.3), any bucket $s$ that has not reached AVALANCHE_FINALIZATION_SCORE within kMaxBucketPollRounds = 6 ($\sim 1.2\text{–}1.5\text{ s}$) deterministically carries forward its already-finalized parent bucket root $L_{N-1}[s]$ into $L_N$ (deferring bucket $s$'s delta to Epoch $N+1$).
  8. Consequently, network-wide, Epoch $N$ is mathematically guaranteed to finalize $L_N$ within $\le 1.5\text{ s}$ even if individual buckets fail to reconcile in time.

Guarantee 2: Local Node Preemption & Single-Step Cumulative Fast-Forward (L_{N-1} -> L_M) When a Local Node Lags

What if the network quorum finalized $L_N$ in $0.8\text{ s}$ and moved on to Epoch $N+1$ (or even $M = N + 8$ after 10 seconds), while our local node got stuck for 10 seconds trying to reconcile Epoch $N$ (for example, waiting on a dead peer's SHORTTX or recovering from a local network stall)?

  4. Calls PendingCandidateTracker::AbortStaleEpochsBelow(M) to purge all PendingCandidateEntry records, m_waiters_by_short_id entries, and in-flight GETSHORTTX / GETMERKLIXBUCK requests for all epochs $\le M$.

  7.   8. Therefore, a node lagging at $L_{N-1}$ when $L_M$ is finalized never replays Epochs $N, N+1, \dots, M$ sequentially.
  9. Instead, it switches m_active_recon_epoch directly to M, runs one single cumulative merklixsync reconciliation between its local state ($L_{N-1}$ + validated transactions already sitting in its local speculative mempool $L_{\text{spec}}$) and $L_M$:
  10. Because P2P transaction relay continues in parallel with Avalanche polling, 99%+ of the transactions in $L_M \setminus L_{N-1}$ are already validated in the lagging node's local CTxMemPool (L_spec)!
  11. Problem 3: Progressively Overlayed Mempools with Low-Cost Merklix TxRoot and UtxoRoot Updates

3.1 Why Key-Addressed Merklix Trees Are $O(\Delta \log N)$ While Positional Merkle Trees Are $O(N)$

This is the foundational data-structure insight of the Lotus V2 architecture:

  • In Bitcoin / Bitcoin Cash (Positional Array-Split Merkle Tree, even with CTOR):
  • Suppose a mempool template $L_1$ has $N = 100{,}000$ transactions sorted by TxID (vtx[1 .. 100,000]).
  • Now a new batch $\Delta_2$ of $m = 4{,}000$ transactions arrives. Because their TxIDs are uniformly distributed across $[0, 2^{256}-1)$, inserting those $4{,}000$ transactions in CTOR order shifts the array index idx of almost every single one of the original $100{,}000$ transactions!
  • Shifting an element's array index from even to odd (or across power-of-two boundaries) invalidates its parent hash at layer 0, which invalidates layer 1, and so on—forcing a 100% full rebuild of all $104{,}000$ leaves ($O(N)$ hashes) on every single template update!
  • In Lotus V2 (Prefix-Keyed Single-Leaf-Collapsed Merklix Trees for Subtree A and UtxoRoot):
  • Both Subtree A (keyed by 256-bit TxID) and UtxoRoot (keyed by OutpointKey = txid || vout) are radix tries (patricia / compressed binary tries) over fixed bit-paths.
  • A transaction's position in Subtree A is determined solely by the bits of its own TxID, never by how many other transactions exist to its left or right.
  • Therefore, inserting $m = |\Delta_i|$ new transactions into a template of $N$ existing transactions touches only the $m$ root-to-leaf paths of the newly inserted transactions!
  • Every unmodified subtree is 100% structurally shared (zero copies, zero re-hashes) between Layer $L_{i-1}$ and Layer $L_i$. Updating Subtree A and UtxoRoot from $L_{i-1} \to L_i$ requires only $O(m \log(N/m))$ node allocations and BLAKE3 hashes!

3.2 Multi-Layer Persistent (Copy-on-Write / Functional) Merklix Overlay Stack

To support $k \approx 24$ progressive epoch overlays ($L_0, L_1, \dots, L_k$) plus a speculative mempool tail ($L_{\text{spec}}$)—and to allow instant rollback or switching if an epoch batch is re-polled or a block arrives at an earlier overlay $L_j$ ($j \le k$)—Lotus V2 uses a Persistent (Functional / Copy-on-Write) Radix-Merklix Trie backed by a cache-aligned slab arena (MerklixOverlayArena).

3.3 Concrete C++ Data Structures for the Overlay Stack

1. Compact 64-Byte Cache-Line-Aligned TxOverlayNode (Subtree A)

Every internal and leaf node of Subtree A in the overlay arena fits in two 64-byte CPU cache lines (128 bytes)—or one 64-byte cache line when child hashes are fetched via child node indices:

namespace lotus::v2::overlay {

// 32-bit arena index. High bit (0x80000000) distinguishes special states.
using NodeIdx = uint32_t;
constexpr NodeIdx kEmptyNodeIdx = 0;

// Exactly 64 bytes (1 CPU cache line) per node in TxOverlayArena!
struct alignas(64) TxOverlayNode {
    Hash digest;               // 32B: TxLeaf(TxID, TxHash) if count==1, else TxMerklixNode hash
    Hash key_or_txid;          // 32B: Full TxID if leaf (count==1); prefix bits if branch (count>1)
    NodeIdx left{0};           //  4B: Left child index in TxOverlayArena (0 if empty/leaf)
    NodeIdx right{0};          //  4B: Right child index in TxOverlayArena (0 if empty/leaf)
    uint32_t count{0};         //  4B: Total transactions in this subtree (1 = collapsed leaf)
    uint16_t split_depth{0};   //  2B: Bit depth d (0..255) for branch, or 256 for leaf
    uint16_t epoch_born{0};    //  2B: Overlay layer index i that allocated this node
    uint32_t pool_tx_idx{0};   //  4B: Index into MempoolTxStore if leaf (gives O(1) access to TxHash/bytes)
    uint32_t reserved{0};      //  4B: Alignment padding -> 88B (or 64B if key_or_txid is looked up via pool_tx_idx)
};

Optimization: Because a leaf (count == 1) already has pool_tx_idx pointing to the immutable MempoolTxEntry (which stores TxID, TxHash, fee, cost_units, and size_bytes), TxOverlayNode does not need to duplicate key_or_txid inside the node! Dropping key_or_txid makes TxOverlayNode exactly 56 bytes (padded to 64 bytes = 1 CPU cache line):

  • digest (32 B)
  • left (4 B), right (4 B)
  • count (4 B), pool_tx_idx (4 B)
  • split_depth (2 B), epoch_born (2 B), flags (4 B), subtree_fee_delta / padding (8 B) $\implies$ 64 bytes.

2. Persistent UtxoOverlayNode with Value-Copied libmdbx Summaries & txid || vout Co-Location

For UtxoRoot, the base UTXO set in libmdbx (dbi_smt_nodes and dbi_coins) may hold $10^8$ coins (~3–8 GB on disk), of which a 120-second block touches at most $\sim 2 \times 10^5$ outpoints ($0.2\%$).

  • Therefore, the in-memory UtxoOverlayArena never loads the full UTXO tree into RAM.
  • Instead, any child pointer in UtxoOverlayNode can be in one of three states encoded in its ChildRef:
  • EMPTY (kind == 0): Empty subtree (EmptyUtxoRoot()).
  • ARENA_IDX (bit 31 == 0): Points to a dirty/path-copied UtxoOverlayNode in RAM allocated by one of layers $L_1 \dots L_k$.
  • MDBX_CLEAN (bit 31 == 1): Unmodified subtree since $L_0$ (dbi_smt_nodes at path.Child(bit).Key()). The parent node in RAM caches a by-value copy of the child's (kind, digest) summary (33 bytes) loaded when the parent was first split, so recomputing the parent's UtxoNode(d, left.digest, right.digest) requires ZERO further libmdbx reads for clean siblings!
  • Zero Long-Lived MDBX_TXN_RDONLY Pinning & Zero Dangling mmap Views:
  • In src/lotus_v2/disk_state.cpp, RawGetView returns a raw std::string_view pointing into libmdbx's memory-mapped file under read_txn_. Holding MDBX_TXN_RDONLY open for 120 seconds across epochs would pin old MVCC pages in libmdbx (preventing page reclamation and risking MDBX_MAP_FULL), and any ResizeMap or write commit invalidates raw string_view pointers (SIGSEGV use-after-unmap).
  • Normative libmdbx Lifetime Rule: MerklixOverlayArena NEVER stores a std::string_view or raw pointer into libmdbx's mmap across an epoch transition. During the ~0.45 ms batch lookup pass of Epoch $e$, each worker acquires a short-lived MDBX_TXN_RDONLY, copies the 33-byte (kind, digest) sibling summary by value into CleanSummaryArena and copies each spent/split Coin ($\le 868\text{ B}$) by value into CoinRecordArena, and immediately calls ResetReadTxn() (mdbx_txn_reset) as soon as the 0.45 ms read pass completes!
// 64-byte cache-line-aligned persistent UTXO Merklix node in UtxoOverlayArena
struct alignas(64) UtxoOverlayNode {
    Hash digest;               // 32B: UtxoLeaf(key, coin) if kind==1, else UtxoNode(d, l, r)
    uint32_t left_ref{0};      //  4B: Arena index (if dirty) or 0x80000000 | clean_summary_idx (by-value copy)
    uint32_t right_ref{0};     //  4B: Arena index (if dirty) or 0x80000000 | clean_summary_idx (by-value copy)
    uint32_t coin_arena_idx{0};//  4B: If kind==1 (singleton leaf), index into by-value CoinRecordArena
    uint16_t depth{0};         //  2B: Bit depth d (0..255)
    uint8_t  kind{0};          //  1B: 0 = Empty, 1 = Singleton Leaf, 2 = Branch
    uint8_t  left_kind{0};     //  1B: Cached Summary.kind (0, 1, 2) of left child
    uint8_t  right_kind{0};    //  1B: Cached Summary.kind (0, 1, 2) of right child
    uint8_t  epoch_born{0};    //  1B: Overlay layer index (0..255)
    uint16_t tx_sub_count{0};  //  2B: Number of outputs sharing this txid prefix (for depth <= 256)
    uint8_t  padding[12];      // 12B: Pad to 64 bytes (1 cache line)
};

Why OutpointKey = txid || vout Prefix Preservation Is a Massive Multiplier for UtxoRoot Overlays

Under the Maintainer Architecture Direction (P02), OutpointKey preserves the 32-byte txid as bits $0 \dots 255$ and places the 32-bit vout in bits $256 \dots 287$ (or mixes vout only in the trailing bits):

  1. Single Shared Path for Multi-Output Transactions:
  2. Suppose a transaction tx in $\Delta_i$ creates $m_{\text{out}} = 4$ spendable outputs (vout = 0, 1, 2, 3).
  3. In a hashed-outpoint tree (H_outpointkey(txid || vout)), those 4 outputs scatter to 4 completely unrelated 27-level paths in UtxoRoot, requiring $4 \times 27 = 108$ node allocations, $108$ BLAKE3 hashes, and 4 random libmdbx B+Tree page lookups!
  4. With OutpointKey = txid || vout prefix preservation, all 4 outputs share the exact same 256-bit txid prefix!
  5. First, we build the tiny mini-tree of tx's 4 outputs in 3 BLAKE3 hashes ($vout \in \{0,1,2,3\}$).
  6. Then, we insert that single mini-tree into utxo_trie_root along one single path of depth $k \approx 25$ (~25 node allocations, ~25 BLAKE3 hashes, and 1 single libmdbx 4 KB leaf page touch)!
  7. For a 2-output average transaction, this cuts UtxoRoot insertion path copies, libmdbx reads, and BLAKE3 hashes by nearly $2\times$ (and by $10\times$ for batch payout/airdrop transactions with 10+ outputs!).
  8. Co-Located Output Shard Ownership (+Epoch Barrier Input Routing):
  9. The horizontal shard $s \in [0, 2^k - 1]$ that owns tx.TxID in Subtree A also owns the UtxoRoot subtree where all of tx's created outputs (tx.TxID, vout) are inserted, while spent inputs (prev_txid, prev_vout) are routed by prev_txid[0..7] at the global atomic epoch barrier $L_{e-1} \to L_e$ (Section 2.3).

3.4 Concrete Step-by-Step Lifecycle: Base $L_0 \to$ Overlays $L_1 \dots L_k \to$ Speculative $L_{\text{spec}}$

Step 1: Initializing Base Layer $L_0$ When Tip Block $B_h$ Connects

When Block $B_h$ connects at height $h$:

  1. DiskStateStore acquires a short-lived MDBX_TXN_RDONLY snapshot at tip $B_h$, copies the root summaries of dbi_smt_nodes, and releases the read transaction via ResetReadTxn().
  2. Delayed Coinbase UTXO Insertion (P02):
  3. Block $B_h$'s coinbase transaction (B_h.vtx[0]) is now immutable and known to every node.
  4. Its spendable outputs ((flags & 0x02) == 0) are inserted into the empty UtxoOverlayArena on top of $B_h$'s dbi_smt_nodes root to form L_0.utxo_root.
  5. L_0.subtree_a_root is initialized to EmptyTxMerklixRoot (H_txroot(0x02)), and all cumulative counters (total_tx_count, total_body_bytes, total_cost_units, total_fee_atoms, required_burn_floor_atoms) are 0.

Step 2: Enforcing the Dynamic P05 Marginal Fee-Burn Cutoff Before Freezing Cohort $c = e$

In Lotus V2 (P05), MAX_BLOCK_BYTES is replaced by an elastic block cost model governed by the rolling ema_block_cost ($E_n$) and a parabolic mandatory fee-burn floor $\text{BurnFloor}(E_n, C_{\text{block}})$ that grows quadratically once cumulative block cost $C_{\text{block}}$ exceeds the free zone above $E_n$.

  • The Negative Marginal Miner Revenue Hazard: If pre-consensus blindly admitted every low-fee transaction from Cohort $e$ into $L_e$ during a traffic spike ($C(L_e) > 2 E_n$), a transaction paying 1 atom/cu at a point on the parabola where the marginal burn increase is 5 atoms/cu would reduce the miner's net claimable coinbase reward by 4 atoms/cu (or push required_burn_floor_atoms > total_fee_atoms + total_burned_atoms, making $L_e$ consensus-invalid to mine!). Rational miners would be forced to abandon $L_e$, breaking Tier 2 $O(1)$ block relay.
  • Normative Dynamic P05 Marginal Fee-Rate Gate: When assembling candidate transactions from matured Cohort $c = e$ on top of $L_{e-1}$ (at cumulative cost $C_0 = L_{e-1}.\text{total\_cost\_units}$):
  • Candidate transactions in Cohort $c = e$ are evaluated in descending order of effective_fee_per_cu $= \frac{\text{fee\_atoms}(tx) + \text{explicit\_burn\_atoms}(tx)}{\text{cost\_units}(tx)}$ (with deterministic tie-breaking by lowest TxID).
  • For each candidate transaction $tx$ with cost $\Delta C = \text{cost\_units}(tx)$, let the P05 marginal burn increment at cumulative cost $C$ be: $$\Delta \text{Burn}(E_n, C, C + \Delta C) = \text{BurnFloor}(E_n, C + \Delta C) - \text{BurnFloor}(E_n, C)$$
  • Strict Non-Negative Marginal Miner Value Rule: A transaction $tx$ is admitted into Epoch Batch $\Delta_e$ if and only if: $$\text{fee\_atoms}(tx) + \text{explicit\_burn\_atoms}(tx) \ge \Delta \text{Burn}(E_n, C, C + \Delta C)$$ Any transaction in Cohort $c = e$ whose fee rate falls below the current P05 marginal burn slope is automatically deferred to $L_{\text{spec}}$ for Block $h+2$, guaranteeing that every finalized overlay $L_e$ is 100% consensus-valid under P05 and strictly increases net miner revenue!

Step 3: Advancing $L_{i-1} \to L_i$ by Applying Epoch Batch $\Delta_i$ (Sort-Merge Batch Path-Copying)

Instead of inserting each of the $m = |\Delta_i|$ transactions one by one from the root (which would re-copy the top $\log_2 m$ levels $m$ times), we sort the $m$ operations by their 256-bit key (TxID for Subtree A; OutpointKey for UtxoRoot) and apply them in a single recursive top-down divide-and-conquer pass (executed in parallel across the $2^8 = 256$ prefix shards):

// Batch-update a Subtree A shard from L_{i-1} to L_i in O(|ops| * log(N_shard / |ops|))
NodeIdx ApplyTxBatchRecursive(
    TxOverlayArena &arena,
    NodeIdx old_node_idx,
    std::span<const TxBatchOp> sorted_ops, // Pre-sorted by 256-bit TxID
    uint16_t depth,
    uint16_t epoch_id)
{
    if (sorted_ops.empty()) {
        return old_node_idx; // 100% structural sharing: 0 allocations, 0 hashes!
    }
    if (old_node_idx == kEmptyNodeIdx && sorted_ops.size() == 1) {
        // Single-leaf collapse: allocate 1 leaf node directly at current depth!
        return arena.AllocateLeaf(sorted_ops[0], epoch_id);
    }
    // If old_node_idx is a collapsed singleton leaf (count == 1), push it down to split
    // against sorted_ops; otherwise partition sorted_ops at bit `depth` via std::partition_point
    auto split_it = std::partition_point(sorted_ops.begin(), sorted_ops.end(),
        [depth](const TxBatchOp &op) { return !Bit(op.tx_id, depth); });

    std::span<const TxBatchOp> left_ops(sorted_ops.begin(), split_it);
    std::span<const TxBatchOp> right_ops(split_it, sorted_ops.end());

    const TxOverlayNode &old_node = arena.Get(old_node_idx);
    NodeIdx new_left  = ApplyTxBatchRecursive(arena, old_node.left,  left_ops,  depth + 1, epoch_id);
    NodeIdx new_right = ApplyTxBatchRecursive(arena, old_node.right, right_ops, depth + 1, epoch_id);

    // Allocate 1 path-copied branch node (caching count = c_L + c_R in RAM) and compute 1 BLAKE3 hash
    // matching P02 commitment.cpp::TxNodeAtDepth: H_txroot(0x01 || u16(depth) || root_L || root_R)
    return arena.AllocateBranch(depth, new_left, new_right, epoch_id);
}

Notice the power of Sort-Merge Batch Path-Copying:

  • The top $\log_2 m$ levels of the tree ($d = 0 \dots 11$ for $m = 4{,}096$) are traversed and allocated only once per batch, not once per transaction!
  • For $m = 4{,}096$ transactions inserted into a tree of $N = 100{,}000$ transactions:
  • The top 12 levels contain at most $2^{12} - 1 = 4{,}095$ shared internal nodes.
  • Below depth 12, each of the $4{,}096$ transactions diverges for an average of only $\log_2(100{,}000 / 4{,}096) \approx 4.6$ levels before hitting a collapsed singleton leaf!
  • Total new TxOverlayNodes allocated for the entire $4{,}096$-tx batch $\Delta_i$: $$\text{Nodes}_{\text{SubtreeA}}(\Delta_i) \approx 4{,}095\text{ (top levels)} + 4{,}096 \times (4.6\text{ branch} + 1\text{ leaf}) \approx \mathbf{27{,}000\text{ nodes}}$$
  • Compare this to naive one-by-one insertion ($4{,}096 \times 18 = 73{,}728$ nodes): batch divide-and-conquer cuts both memory allocations and BLAKE3 hashes by $2.7\times$!

  7. As a result, total overlay RAM scales only with the net number of unique transactions in the current block, completely independent of how many epochs ($24, 100, \text{ or } 500$) occur per block!

  9. MerklixOverlayArena grows dynamically via $8\text{ MiB}$ slab chunks proportional to maxmempool and $E_n$ (~$0.95\text{ MiB}$ per $1{,}000$ finalized transactions).

3.6 $O(1)$ Block Receipt via the Candidate Root Match Stage (tmplblock = Header || CoinbaseTx) & Automatic cmpctblock / block Fallback

[!IMPORTANT] Maintainer Question & Architectural Simplification: "Is there block relay messages that don't include txns? I thought the node streamed the whole block or if it supported compact blocks, it did that and then those pulled out of the mempool. Is there a stage in the state where a block's header can simply check the roots against the candidate blocks? If not that needs to be added, if so, then nodes that are behind or whatever can simply fallback to compact blocks or full block transfer?"

1. What Exists in lotusd Today vs. What Must Be Added

Today, lotusd (src/net_processing.cpp, src/blockencodings.cpp) only has three block-relay paths, none of which can connect a non-empty block without transmitting a per-transaction list:

  1. headers (NetMsgType::HEADERS): Transmits only the 134-byte Header. Because Header.tx_root = H_txroot(0x03 || CoinbaseLeaf || SubtreeA_Root) combines the miner's CoinbaseTx (vtx[0]) with Subtree A (vtx[1..n-1]), a node receiving only Header lacks CoinbaseTx and therefore cannot even test whether Header.tx_root matches its local pre-consensus overlay $L_k$!
  2. cmpctblock (NetMsgType::CMPCTBLOCK): Transmits Header + nonce + 6-byte short_id for EVERY transaction + prefilled CoinbaseTx. For a $100{,}000$-transaction block, cmpctblock still transmits $6\text{ B} \times 100{,}000 = 600\text{ KB}$ of short IDs and forces the receiver to allocate and match a $100{,}000$-entry table!
  3. block (NetMsgType::BLOCK): Streams the full multi-megabyte serialized block.

What We Add: We introduce a Transaction-Free Block Announcement (tmplblock = Header || CoinbaseTx, ~284 bytes total) and a Stage 1 Candidate Root Match Stage (TryConnectFromCandidateOverlays) in src/net_processing.cpp that checks Header.tx_root and Header.state_root directly against the node's in-memory ring buffer of candidate overlays $\{L_k, L_{k-1}, L_{k-2}, L_{\text{spec}}\}$ in $< 5\text{ }\mu\text{s}$—and if no candidate overlay matches (because the node is behind, has Avalanche disabled, or the miner included custom unfinalized transactions), automatically falls back to standard getdata(MSG_CMPCT_BLOCK) or getdata(MSG_BLOCK)!

2. How a Miner Constructs a Block Header from $L_k$ (or $L_{\text{spec}}$) in $O(1)$

When getblocktemplate runs (or whenever $L_k$ advances):

  1. The miner constructs its own coinbase transaction vtx[0] claiming up to S_n + L_k.total_fee_atoms - max(0, required_burn - L_k.total_burned_atoms) (P05/P06) and computes CoinbaseLeaf = H_txroot(0x00 || coinbase_txid || coinbase_txhash).
  2. The miner computes header.tx_root in 1 single BLAKE3 hash ($O(1)$, ~65 ns) without touching any shard: $$\text{header.tx\_root} = H_{\text{txroot}}(\texttt{0x03} \parallel \text{CoinbaseLeaf} \parallel L_k.\text{subtree\_a\_root})$$
  3. Because Block $h+1$'s coinbase outputs are pipelined into UtxoRoot in Block $h+2$ (P02), L_k.utxo_root is ALREADY the exact final UtxoRoot of Block $h+1$!
  4. The miner updates the 56-byte MonetaryState ($R_{n+1}, T_{n+1}, W_n, E_{n+1}$) using $C_{\text{block}} = C_{\text{coinbase}} + L_k.\text{total\_cost\_units}$ and computes header.state_root in 1 single 130-byte BLAKE3 hash ($O(1)$, ~100 ns): $$\text{header.state\_root} = H_{\text{state}}(\texttt{0x00} \parallel \text{ChainID} \parallel \text{u64}(h+1) \parallel L_k.\text{utxo\_root} \parallel \texttt{0x38} \parallel \text{MonetaryState}_{n+1})$$
  5. header.size is simply $134 + 4 + \text{framed\_len}(\text{vtx}[0]) + L_k.\text{total\_body\_bytes}$.

  16. Flattening Matched Overlay $L$ to libmdbx (dbi_coins and dbi_smt_nodes):
  17. Only the net difference between $L_0$ and $L$ needs to be written to libmdbx:
  18. Any node in UtxoOverlayArena reachable from L.utxo_root with epoch_born >= 1 is emitted as a Put('N' || bit_rev_prefix || depth_u16le, EncodeNode(node)) into MdbxWriteBatch.
  19. Any node in $L_0$ that was replaced or collapsed in $L$ without a survivor at its Path::Key() is emitted as a Delete('N' || ...).

Problem 4: Bootstrap & Graceful Fallback When No Avalanche Nodes Are Present

[!IMPORTANT] Maintainer Question: "How does this fall back early on when there are no Avalanche nodes?"

A critical systems requirement for Lotus V2 is Zero Hard-Consensus Coupling between the Avalanche pre-consensus overlay protocol and deterministic Proof-of-Work block validity. The system must operate seamlessly—and still gain massive $O(|\text{Block} \triangle L_{\text{spec}}| \log N)$ Merklix overlay speedups—from Block 0 (genesis bootstrap, regtest, solo mining, or periods without an Avalanche quorum) all the way to full-quorum pre-consensus.

4.1 Zero Consensus Coupling in the 134-Byte Block Header

In Lotus V2 (P04), the 134-byte block header commits only to tx_root (32 bytes) and state_root (32 bytes):

Header = prev_id:h32 || bits:u32 || time:u48 || nonce:u64
         || version:u32(2) || size:u64 || height:u64
         || tx_root:h32 || state_root:h32                     # 134 bytes
  • The header contains no OverlayID, no vector clock, no epoch counter, and no Avalanche quorum certificate.
  • tx_root ($H_{\text{txroot}}(\texttt{0x03} \parallel \text{CoinbaseLeaf} \parallel \text{SubtreeA})$) and state_root ($H_{\text{state}}(\dots \parallel \text{UtxoRoot} \parallel \text{MonetaryState})$) are pure deterministic functions of the ordered transaction set in the block.
  • Therefore, block validity is 100% identical whether 0 nodes, 5 nodes, or 10,000 nodes are running Avalanche.

  1.   2.   3. Lotus V2's Bijective (Invertible) Merklix Advantage:
  4. Section 5: Adversarial Threat Model, Attack Mitigations, and Protocol Hardening

To ensure the protocol is resilient against Byzantine peers, cryptographic grinding, P2P eclipse/churn manipulation, economic fee-burn griefing, resource exhaustion, and fast-sync spoofing, this section systematically catalogs 16 red-team attack surfaces (Holes 1–10 for Bulk Pre-Consensus & Merklix Overlays, plus Holes 11–16 for Fast UTXO Shard Sync & Reverse Block Peeling) and the normative protocol defenses baked into the specification.

5.1 Hole 1 — 64-Bit Truncated root64 Birthday Forgery & Algebraic Syndrome Spoofing

  • Attack Vector:
  • Birthday Root Forgery: If sub-bucket Merklix roots were truncated to 64 bits (root64[s]: u64), a Byzantine attacker could grind two sets of valid transactions $S_1 \ne S_2$ in bucket $s$ whose 64-bit truncated Merklix roots collide in $2^{32} \approx 4.3 \times 10^9$ BLAKE3 evaluations (< 0.5 seconds on a consumer GPU). Two halves of the network would believe bucket $s$ converged while computing incompatible 256-bit SubtreeA_Roots.
  • Unkeyed SipHash / 3-Tx Syndrome Aliasing: With a static or predictable SipHash key, an attacker can grind two transactions with identical 64-bit short_ids ($x_1 = x_2 \implies \Delta s_1 = 0, \Delta s_3 = 0$), or construct a 3-transaction difference $(x_1, x_2, x_3)$ where $(x_1 \oplus x_2 \oplus x_3)^3 \equiv x_1^3 \oplus x_2^3 \oplus x_3^3 \pmod{P_{64}(X)}$ so it mimics a 1-transaction difference $x_{\text{fake}} = x_1 \oplus x_2 \oplus x_3$.
  • Normative Protocol Defense (Sections 1.2, 1.3, 1.5):
  • Zero Root Truncation: All Merklix bucket root comparisons use full 256-bit (32-byte) root[s]: h32 ($128\text{-bit}$ birthday security). Because Adaptive Top-Down Polling (Section 1.5) only transmits dirty/mismatched subtrees, sending full 32-byte roots costs zero extra bytes in the 99% root-match case and only $+24\text{ B}$ per dirty bucket on mismatch.
  • Per-Epoch Unpredictable SipHash Key: $(k_0^{(e)}, k_1^{(e)}) = \text{BLAKE3\_Keyed\_128}(\texttt{"Lotus/v2/preconsensus/sipkey"}, B_h.\text{BlockID} \parallel \text{u32}(e) \parallel L_{e-1}.\text{overlay\_id})$, preventing pre-epoch short_id collision grinding.
  • Strict 256-Bit Root-Verification Gate: Any short_id decoded from $(\Delta s_1, \Delta s_3)$ is treated strictly as an untrusted lookup hint; a node accepts a bucket transition only after resolving and validating the full transaction(s), recomputing the 256-bit TxMerklixNode hash, and verifying recomputed_root_32B[s] == peer_root_32B[s]. Any mismatch immediately triggers deterministic 256-bit Merklix child bisection.

5.2 Hole 2 — Backdated & Future-Dated Cohort Tag Spoofing on P2P Relay

  • Attack Vector: If P2P transaction relay carried a sender-supplied (cohort = e, Tx) tag, a Byzantine node could withhold 5,000 transactions until 10 ms before Epoch $e+2$ begins voting on Cohort $e$, then broadcast them with a backdated tag cohort = e to half the network (causing a 50/50 split on Cohort $e$'s candidate set), or broadcast transactions with a future-dated tag cohort = e + 100 to poison future cohorts.
  • Normative Protocol Defense (Section 2.2):
  • Zero Wire-Cohort Trust: P2P inv and tx messages carry no cohort tag on the wire.
  • Local Receive-Epoch Stamping (local_cohort(T) = e_local): Each node stamps a transaction with its own local logical epoch e_local at the instant the transaction passes local mempool validation.
  • Maturation Barrier Immunity: Honestly broadcast transactions in Epoch $e$ propagate in $\sim 150\text{ ms}$ and are locally validated by all honest nodes either during Epoch $e$ (local_cohort = e) or early in Epoch $e+1$ (local_cohort = e+1, well before Epoch $e+2$ voting starts). Conversely, a last-millisecond dump at the end of Epoch $e+1$ arrives after honest nodes have advanced to $e_{\text{local}} \ge e+1$ (or $e+2$) and is blocked from disrupting Cohort $e$ by the Pre-Cutoff Eligibility Gate.

5.3 Hole 3 — Cross-Shard UTXO Spend Coupling vs. Asynchronous Bucket Clocks

  • Attack Vector: While Subtree A is partitioned by tx.TxID prefix $s \in [0..255]$ and created outputs (OutpointKey = txid || vout) land in the same UtxoRoot prefix shard $s$, spent inputs (prev_txid || prev_vout) delete UTXOs from arbitrary other UtxoRoot shards $s' \ne s$. If buckets advanced their UTXO states at independent asynchronous revisions without a global epoch barrier, cross-bucket unconfirmed spends and cross-shard input deletions would cause combinatorial UtxoRoot divergence across peers.
  • Normative Protocol Defense (Section 2.3):
  • Cross-Bucket Dependency Invariant: A transaction $T$ is eligible for Cohort $c = e$ if and only if all of its spent outpoints already exist in the previously finalized global overlay $L_e$ (or Base $L_0$). Unconfirmed children spending parents finalized in $L_e$ automatically enter Cohort $e+1$ (~$0.5\text{–}1.0\text{ s}$ later), eliminating all intra-epoch cross-bucket causal dependencies.
  • Separation of Intra-Epoch Bucket Convergence ($\mathbf{B}_e$) from Global Atomic Epoch Commit ($L_e$): Within Epoch $e+2$, the 256 Subtree A buckets reconcile in parallel via $\mathbf{B}_e$ (with an 8-round deferral cap so a contended bucket simply keeps $L_{e-1}[s]$ and never stalls other buckets). Once $\mathbf{B}_e$ locks $\Delta_e = \bigcup_s \Delta_{e,s}$, all spent input deletions and created output insertions are applied in a single atomic barrier transition $L_{e-1} \to L_e$ across all 256 UtxoRoot shards.

5.4 Hole 4 — The "Drip-Feed Superset Stall" Liveness Attack

  • Attack Vector: During Stage 3 bucket polling, an adversary broadcasts 1 new valid transaction per poll round ($r = 1, 2, \dots$) into bucket $s$ and advertises a candidate root with count = k + r. If honest nodes unconditionally switched to any higher-count valid superset, the adversary could reset Avalanche confidence to 0 on every round and stall bucket $s$ indefinitely.
  • Normative Protocol Defense (Section 2.4):
  • Gate 1 — Pre-Cutoff Local Eligibility Gate (local_cohort(T_x) <= e + 1): When voting on Cohort $c = e$ during Epoch $e+2$, a node ONLY promotes a missing transaction $T_x$ into a peer's higher-count superset candidate if $T_x$ was already validated in the node's local mempool prior to the start of Epoch $e+2$ voting. Transactions arriving mid-vote (local_cohort >= e + 2) cannot trigger superset switches.
  • Gate 2 — Confidence Lock-In Ceiling (kSupersetLockConfidence = 3): Once a candidate bucket root accumulates $\ge 3$ consecutive positive polls, the node locks its preference and refuses count-based superset jumps for the remainder of that epoch.

5.5 Hole 5 — Zero-Cost Double-Spend Censorship (ConflictSet[o] Withholding Grief)

  • Attack Vector: Victim broadcasts $T_A$ spending outpoint $o$ during Stage 1 (Cohort $e$). Attacker observes $T_A$ on P2P and broadcasts a conflicting transaction $T_B$ spending $o$ during Stage 2 (Epoch $e+1$). If the presence of $T_B$ caused honest nodes to withhold both $T_A$ and $T_B$ in ConflictSet[o] during Epoch $e+2$, the attacker could censor $T_A$ for zero fee cost (since neither $T_A$ nor $T_B$ is mined).
  • Normative Protocol Defense (Section 2.6.B):
  • Cohort Seniority / First-Seen Maturation Lock: Once Cohort $c = e$ closes at the end of Stage 1 (L_e finalizes), any transaction $T_A$ admitted into Cohort $c = e$ holds Cohort Seniority over outpoint $o$. A conflicting transaction $T_B$ arriving in a later cohort (local_cohort(T_B) >= e + 1) cannot displace $T_A$ or trigger ConflictSet[o] withholding for Cohort $e$. Only conflicts arriving within the same Stage 1 cohort window (local_cohort(T_A) == local_cohort(T_B) == e) enter ConflictSet[o] polling.

5.6 Hole 6 — P05 Parabolic Block Burn Floor & Negative Marginal Miner Revenue Attack

  • Attack Vector: Under Lotus V2's elastic block size model (P05), mandatory fee burn $\text{BurnFloor}(E_n, C_{\text{block}})$ increases quadratically with cumulative block cost $C_{\text{block}}$. Flooding the mempool with minimum-fee transactions during a burst could push the marginal P05 burn cost per cu above those transactions' fee rates—reducing the miner's net claimable reward for every extra transaction added to $L_e$ (or making $L_e$ violate the P05 burn floor).
  • Normative Protocol Defense (Section 3.4, Step 2):
  • Dynamic P05 Marginal Fee-Rate Gate: Before freezing Cohort $c = e$ on top of $L_{e-1}$, candidate transactions are sorted by descending effective_fee_per_cu and admitted into $\Delta_e$ only while $\text{fee}(tx) + \text{explicit\_burn}(tx) \ge \Delta \text{Burn}(E_n, C, C + \text{cu}(tx))$. Transactions below the current parabolic marginal burn slope remain in $L_{\text{spec}}$ for future blocks, guaranteeing that every finalized overlay $L_e$ is consensus-valid and maximizes net miner revenue.

5.7 Hole 7 — TemplateBlockAnnounce (tmplblock) Pre-PoW Allocation DoS & Candidate Root Miss Handling

  • Attack Vector:
  • Sending a fake tmplblock (Header || CoinbaseTx) with an invalid PoW header and a multi-megabyte bogus CoinbaseTx payload to trigger memory allocation or hashing work before PoW is checked.
  • A node that is lagging behind, has Avalanche disabled, or receives a block mined by a miner with custom unfinalized transactions does not have a matching candidate overlay root in $\{L_k, L_{k-1}, L_{k-2}, L_0, L_{\text{spec}}\}$.
  • Normative Protocol Defense (Section 3.6 & Section 4.2):
  • Strict Pre-Allocation Header PoW & Coinbase Size Gate: Nodes verify the 134-byte Header PoW (BlockID <= target) and Header.size bounds before parsing CoinbaseTx, and cap CoinbaseTx deserialization at kMaxCoinbaseBytes ($\le 10\text{ KB}$).
  • Two-Stage Candidate Root Check $\to$ Automatic cmpctblock / block Fallback: TryConnectFromCandidateOverlays checks H_txroot(0x03 || CoinbaseLeaf || L.subtree_a_root) == Header.tx_root across $\{L_k, L_{k-1}, L_{k-2}, L_0, L_{\text{spec}}\}$ in $< 5\text{ }\mu\text{s}$. If no local candidate overlay matches, the node accepts the PoW-verified Header into BlockIndex and immediately sends standard getdata(MSG_CMPCT_BLOCK, BlockID) (pulling transactions from the mempool and validating via symmetric diff against $L_{\text{spec}}$) or getdata(MSG_BLOCK, BlockID) (full block transfer when behind tip).

5.8 Hole 8 — libmdbx Long-Lived MDBX_TXN_RDONLY Pinning & Dangling mmap Views

  • Attack Vector: Holding MDBX_TXN_RDONLY open across all 120 seconds of an overlay lifecycle pins historical MVCC pages in libmdbx (preventing garbage collection under churn) and exposes UtxoOverlayNode to SIGSEGV use-after-unmap crashes if RawGetView std::string_view pointers into the mmap are retained across a map resize or write transaction.
  • Normative Protocol Defense (Section 3.3.2 & 3.4):
  • By-Value Arena Copy + Immediate ResetReadTxn(): MerklixOverlayArena never retains raw pointers or std::string_views into libmdbx. During each ~0.45 ms epoch read pass, worker threads copy the 33-byte (kind, digest) sibling summaries by value into CleanSummaryArena and copy spent/split Coin bytes ($\le 868\text{ B}$) by value into CoinRecordArena, immediately calling ResetReadTxn() (mdbx_txn_reset) when the read pass finishes.

5.9 Hole 9 — Speculative Poll Arena Exhaustion vs. Elastic P05 Block Size Scaling

  • Attack Vector: A Byzantine peer sends thousands of avapoll messages advertising distinct unfinalized bucket roots to force the victim to allocate copy-on-write UtxoOverlayNode trees in RAM until OOM; conversely, if the victim defends itself with a naive hard RAM cap (e.g., 128 MiB) on finalized overlays, that cap would artificially limit maximum block size in violation of P05's elastic block cost design!
  • Normative Protocol Defense (Section 3.5.B):
  • Zero UtxoOverlayArena Allocation on Peer Polls (0 Bytes): Unfinalized peer polls operate strictly on Subtree A (TxRoot) hashes over MempoolTxStore. UtxoOverlayArena is never mutated or branched for unfinalized poll candidates—advancing strictly once per finalized epoch $L_{e-1} \to L_e$.
  • In-Place Epoch Folding ($L_{e-3} \to L_{\text{base}}$): Superseded interior nodes from epochs $\le e - 3$ are recycled immediately to per-shard freelists in $O(1)$, so RAM scales only with net unique block transactions, not epoch count.
  • Elastic RAM Scaling + libmdbx Staged Checkpoint Spillover: If an ultra-large elastic block ($10^6+$ txs) ever exceeds a node's configured -maxoverlayram cache watermark, folded $L_{\text{base}}$ subtrees flush to dbi_staged_coins / dbi_staged_smt in libmdbx and free their RAM slabs—guaranteeing zero artificial limit on maximum block size.

5.10 Hole 10 — Overlapping Epoch Reconciliation & Lagging-Node State Pileup (10-Second Stall Scenario)

  • Failure / Attack Vector: What happens if Epoch $N$ takes 10 seconds to reconcile on a node (due to slow GETSHORTTX responses, heavy packet loss, or adversarial delay), while Epoch $N+1$ (or $M > N$) is started? Concurrent reconciliation across overlapping epochs would corrupt parent UTXO / P05 burn-floor assumptions, use undefined SipHash keys, and multiply in-flight fetch queues.
  • Normative Protocol Defense (Section 2.7 & Section 6.4):
  • Network-Wide Completion-Clocked Single-Flight Invariant + 6-Round Bucket Cap (<= 1.5s): Epochs are never started by a wall-clock timer; Epoch $N+1$ requires parent_overlay_id = L_N.overlay_id and derives its SipHash key from L_N.overlay_id, so the quorum cannot start Epoch $N+1$ until $L_N$ finalizes. Furthermore, kMaxBucketPollRounds = 6 (~1.2–1.5s) forces any non-converged bucket $s$ to carry forward $L_{N-1}[s]$ into $L_N$, preventing Epoch $N$ from ever stalling network-wide.
  • Local Preemption (AbortStaleEpochsBelow(M)) + Single-Step Cumulative Fast-Forward ($L_{N-1} \to L_M$): Each node maintains at most one active reconciliation epoch (m_active_recon_epoch). If a lagging local node stuck at $L_{N-1}$ sees via CausalEpochHeader that the quorum has already finalized $L_M$ ($M \ge N$), it immediately aborts and purges all pending reconciliation state for Epoch $N$ (AbortStaleEpochsBelow(M)), performs one single cumulative merklixsync diff directly between $L_{N-1}$ and $L_M$ (resolving 99%+ of transactions from its local L_spec), commits $L_{N-1} \to L_M$ in a single batch path-copy, and joins Epoch $M+1$!

5.11 Hole 11 — Fast-Sync Prefix-Shard Boundary Injection & Non-Canonical Single-Leaf Collapse Spoofing (utxoroots / utxoshard)

  • Attack Vector:
  • Cross-Shard Smuggling: During Phase 2 of Fast UTXO Shard Sync (utxoshard), a Byzantine peer serving shard $s \in [0..255]$ includes a fabricated Coin whose OutpointKey(coin.outpoint) has first byte $s' \ne s$, or sends duplicate OutpointKeys, or sends a burn output ((flags & 0x02) != 0, violating C17). If the receiver wrote coins to DiskStateStore before checking that every key belongs to shard $s$, the attacker could inject unauthenticated UTXOs into other shards!
  • Sparse Single-Leaf Collapse Mismatch Above Depth 8: In a single-leaf-collapsed Merklix tree (commitments-and-state.md:148–152), if an ancestor subtree at depth $d < 8$ has only 1 leaf total across all its descendants, it collapses above depth 8 without hashing UtxoNode(d, leaf, EmptyRoot). A naive depth-8 array of 32-byte hashes without cardinality tags (Empty = 0, SingleLeaf = 1, Interior = 2) would fail to fold to UtxoRoot_P on sparse subtrees or allow a peer to spoof a collapsed leaf as an interior node.
  • Normative Protocol Defense (Section 4.4):
  • Annotated Depth-8 ShardRootEntry (cardinality_tag: u8 || subtree_root: h32): When folding the 256 ShardRootEntry records from depth $d = 7$ down to $d = 0$ against B_P.state_root, the verifier applies the exact P02 single-leaf collapse rule (0 + 0 -> 0, 1 + 0 -> 1 with zero UtxoNode hash, $\ge 2 \to \text{UtxoNode}(d, l, r)$) and rejects any utxoroots message whose folded root + MonetaryState_P does not equal B_P.state_root.
  • Strict In-Memory Per-Shard Verification Before Disk Commit: For every Coin in utxoshard:
  • Recompute key = OutpointKey(coin.outpoint) locally (never trust a wire key).
  • Require prefix_bits(key, k) == requested_prefix, strictly ascending key order (zero duplicates), (coin.output.flags & 0x02) == 0 (C17 spendable only), coin.creation_height <= P, and valid output structure.
  • Reconstruct the shard's Merklix subtree in RAM and verify (cardinality_tag, root) == verified_shard_root[s] before writing a single coin to DiskStateStore!

5.12 Hole 12 — Intra-Block Spend (O_h \cap I_h) Confusion & Fake BlockUndo Spoofing During Reverse Block Peeling

  • Attack Vector:
  • During Phase 3 (Reverse Block Peeling from $h = P$ down to $h = 1$), the node is at verified state $(\text{UtxoRoot}_h, \text{MonetaryState}_h)$ and downloads (B_h, BlockUndo_h) to step backwards to $h - 1$.
  • Suppose block $B_h$ contains an intra-block parent-child spend: transaction $T_{\text{parent}} \in B_h$ creates outpoint op_ephem, and transaction $T_{\text{child}} \in B_h$ spends op_ephem in the same block.
  • Because op_ephem was created and consumed inside $B_h$, op_ephem exists in neither $\text{UtxoRoot}_{h-1}$ nor $\text{UtxoRoot}_h$! If the reverse peeling logic failed to separate intra-block spends ($I_h^{\text{intra}}$) from prior-block spends ($I_h^{\text{prior}}$) and inserted op_ephem into $\text{UtxoRoot}'_{h-1}$ (or allowed BlockUndo_h to supply a fake Coin for op_ephem), $\text{UtxoRoot}'_{h-1}$ would be corrupted or T_child would be validated against a spoofed Coin!
  • Normative Protocol Defense (Section 4.4):
  • Exact Partition of $O_h^{\text{net}}$, $I_h^{\text{intra}}$, and $I_h^{\text{prior}}$:
  • From $B_h$ and the validated header chain (creation_height = h, creation_parent_mtp = MTP(h-1)), construct the map $O_h^{\text{all}}$ of all spendable outputs created in $B_h$.
  • Partition every spent input outpoint op in $B_h$ (vtx[1..n-1]):
  • If $\text{op} \in O_h^{\text{all}}$, it is an intra-block spend ($I_h^{\text{intra}}$): its Coin is resolved 100% locally from $O_h^{\text{all}}$ (never read from BlockUndo_h), and op is removed from $O_h^{\text{all}}$ to yield the net surviving outputs $O_h^{\text{net}} = O_h^{\text{all}} \setminus I_h^{\text{intra}}$.
  • If $\text{op} \notin O_h^{\text{all}}$, it is a prior-block spend ($I_h^{\text{prior}}$): its Coin is consumed from BlockUndo_h, which MUST have coin.creation_height < h.
  • Pre-Execution Bijective StateRoot Gate:
  • Verify every coin in $O_h^{\text{net}}$ exists with identical bytes in $\text{UtxoRoot}_h$, and no outpoint in $I_h^{\text{prior}}$ exists in $\text{UtxoRoot}_h$.
  • Compute $\text{UtxoRoot}'_{h-1} = \text{SmtInsertBatch}(\text{SmtEraseBatch}(\text{UtxoRoot}_h, O_h^{\text{net}}), I_h^{\text{prior}})$ and verify: $$H_{\text{state}}(\texttt{0x00} \parallel \text{ChainID} \parallel (h-1) \parallel \text{UtxoRoot}'_{h-1} \parallel \texttt{0x38} \parallel \text{MonetaryState}_{h-1}) == \text{Header}_{h-1}.\text{state\_root}$$ Because Header_{h-1}.state_root is already fixed in the PoW-authenticated header chain, a peer cannot forge a single bit of any Coin in $I_h^{\text{prior}}$ or $\text{MonetaryState}_{h-1}$.
  • Finally, run full V2 transaction, signature, VM script, and P05/P06 monetary transition validation on $B_h$ against $I_h^{\text{prior}} \cup I_h^{\text{intra}}$.

5.13 Hole 13 — Unfinalized / Shallow-Fork Pivot Spoofing During Fast Sync

  • Attack Vector: An eclipse or minority-hashrate attacker mines a 1-block private fork $B_{\text{fake}}$ with an invalid state transition (minting arbitrary coins in B_fake.state_root) and tries to make a newly booting node fast-sync to $B_{\text{fake}}$.
  • Normative Protocol Defense (Section 4.4):
  • Dual Pivot Authentication Gate: A node only accepts a fast-sync pivot $B_P$ if:
  • The active header chain satisfies ChainWork(Tip) >= nMinimumChainWork, AND
  • Either $B_P$ is confirmed finalized by the live Avalanche stake quorum (isQuorumEstablished() && isFinalized(B_P)), OR $B_P$ is buried by at least kFastSyncPivotFinalityDepth = 100 valid-PoW headers on the best header chain and its header is confirmed by $\ge 3$ outbound peers across distinct /16 netgroups.
  • Furthermore, because Phase 3 (Reverse Block Peeling) peels every block from $B_P$ down to Genesis (height = 0), any historical invalid state transition is deterministically caught when the reverse cursor reaches that block.

5.14 Hole 14 — Tip Reorg Below Pivot $B_P$ Before Reverse Peeling Has Downloaded Undo Data

  • Attack Vector: If a node fast-synced directly to the unfinalized tip $B_{\text{tip}}$ without undo data for $B_{\text{tip}}$, and a 2-block reorg arrived 1 second later before Reverse Block Peeling had downloaded BlockUndo_{tip}, DisconnectTip() would lack the undo record needed to roll back $B_{\text{tip}}$.
  • Normative Protocol Defense (Section 4.4):
  • Pivot Safety Cushion + Immediate Top-Window Forward Connect: Pivot $B_P$ is selected at an Avalanche-finalized block (which cannot be reorged under Avalanche finality) or at P = H_tip - kPivotSafetyBlocks ($k = 10$), and during Phase 2 the node forward-connects the top $k$ full blocks $B_{P+1} \dots B_{\text{tip}}$ (generating local BlockUndo records for the entire reorg-eligible tip window before going online), while Reverse Peeling immediately downloads and verifies $B_P, B_{P-1}, \dots$ in descending order.

5.15 Hole 15 — Oversized Shard Streaming & Unverified Pagination RAM Exhaustion in utxoshard

  • Attack Vector: If a single depth-8 shard $s$ grew to $500{,}000$ coins (~50 MB, exceeding the 2–16 MiB v2hello message cap) and had to be streamed across 50 unverified pagination chunks, a malicious peer could feed 49 chunks of garbage (~49 MB) before the victim discovered at chunk 50 that the depth-8 Merklix root failed to match.
  • Normative Protocol Defense (Section 4.4):
  • Self-Authenticating Adaptive Depth-$k$ Sub-Shards (k in [8..16], $\le 4{,}096$ Coins / $\le 1\text{ MiB}$): Every utxoshard message is capped at kMaxUtxoShardLeaves = 4,096 coins ($\le 1\text{ MiB}$). Whenever a depth-8 shard exceeds 4,096 coins, it is requested at deeper prefix depth $k \in [9..16]$ and carries the $(k - 8)$ 32-byte Merklix boundary siblings ($\le 256\text{ B}$) linking that sub-shard's root directly to ShardRootEntry[s]. Thus every single $\le 1\text{ MiB}$ utxoshard packet is 100% self-authenticating on arrival with zero multi-packet unverified RAM buffering.

5.16 Hole 16 — Historical RPC & Indexer Queries During Active Reverse Peeling

  • Attack Vector: While the node is online at $H_{\text{tip}}$ and Reverse Peeling is at height $h_{\text{peel}} \in (0, P]$, a client queries getblock or transaction history for an older block at height $h < h_{\text{peel}}$ that has not yet been peeled.
  • Normative Protocol Defense (Section 4.4):
  • Chainstate and lock-free ChainAndMempoolSnapshot track earliest_verified_height atomically (initialized to $P$ at the end of Phase 2, decremented as each block $h$ is reverse-peeled down to 0).
  • getblockchaininfo exposes "fast_sync_active", "pivot_height", and "earliest_verified_height". Tip RPCs, UTXO lookups (gettxout), getblocktemplate, submitrawtxbatch, and block queries for $h \ge \text{earliest\_verified\_height}$ are served at full speed; block body queries for $h < \text{earliest\_verified\_height}$ return a clear "block-pending-reverse-sync" status until earliest_verified_height reaches 0.

Why Set Reconciliation Messages Should Be Generalized Mempool / Merklix Messages (NOT ava*-Specific)

Making the reconciliation messages generalized Merklix mempool-reconciliation messages (merklixsync, getmerklixbuck, merklixbuck, getshorttx, shorttx) rather than ava*-specific messages provides two major architectural advantages:

  1. Strict Separation of Concerns (avalanche::Processor Stays a Pure Hash-Voting Engine):
  2. avalanche::Processor (src/avalanche/processor.h/.cpp) remains strictly a cryptographic voting and confidence engine over 32-byte hashes (avapoll / avaresponse). It never parses transactions, finite-field syndromes, or Merklix radix trees.
  3. All set reconciliation, short_id decoding, transaction fetching, and Merklix tree construction live in CTxMemPool / MerklixOverlayManager (src/lotus_v2/mempool_overlay.h/.cpp) and PeerManagerImpl (src/net_processing.cpp).
  4. One Unified Reconciliation Protocol Across Both Pre-Consensus Overlays and Live Mempool Sync (Including Tier 0 Without Avalanche!):
  5. By prefixing every reconciliation message with a 1-byte ReconScope tag, the exact same 5 messages and C++ handlers power both pre-consensus and general P2P mempool synchronization:
  6. ReconScope::PRECONSENSUS_EPOCH (0x01): Reconciling a candidate epoch overlay (tip_block_id, epoch) when an avapoll / avaresponse vote reveals a difference (or performing a single-step cumulative jump $L_{N-1} \to L_M$ when catching up!).
  7. ReconScope::LIVE_MEMPOOL_SYNC (0x02): Periodic Erlay-style low-bandwidth P2P mempool synchronization against L_spec even in Tier 0 when zero Avalanche nodes exist on the network (e.g., bootstrap blocks $0 \dots 2{,}016$, regtest, or newly connected peers syncing their mempools in 1 RTT without flooding invs)!

B. The 4-State Lifecycle of a Candidate Root in avalanche::VoteRecord and PendingCandidateTracker (Plus Overlapping-Epoch Preemption)

To ensure avalanche::Processor never votes YES on an unvalidated hash, MerklixOverlayManager always has the exact validated transactions needed to build $L_e$ the microsecond a vote finalizes, and no two pre-consensus epochs ever reconcile concurrently, PendingCandidateTracker enforces a strict single-active-epoch state machine:

#### 2. Quorum- & Relative-Stake-Driven Avalanche Activation (Zero Block-Height Magic Numbers)

- **Why Online Quorum + Relative Staked Coins Is Better Than a Hardcoded Block Height:**
  - Using a hardcoded block height (`nAvalancheBootstrapHeight`) is brittle: if a network mines past height 200 before operators have brought enough Avalanche staking nodes online, height-based activation would turn on soft block-parking prematurely. Conversely, on a test or custom network where enough mature stake and Avalanche nodes come online immediately after coinbase maturity, waiting for an arbitrary block height is unnecessary.
  - Because Lotus V2 tracks the exact live **`current_supply`** inside the 56-byte [`MonetaryState`](../../decisions/commitments-and-state.md) committed by every block header (`StateRoot`), and [`avalanche::Processor::isQuorumEstablished()`](file:///private/tmp/lotus-v2-lane-b-mdbx-daemon-20261015/src/avalanche/processor.cpp#L840-L924) already tracks live connected Avalanche nodes and proof scores, we can gate all Avalanche soft policies purely on **live online quorum + relative staked supply**!
- **Exact Three-Condition Quorum Gate in `avalanche::Processor::isQuorumEstablished()` (`src/avalanche/processor.cpp:840–907`):**
  1. **Minimum Online Avalanche Nodes & Proof Inventory Exchange:**
     - `peerManager->getNodeCount() >= minQuorumNodes` (default `8` connected Avalanche nodes across distinct proofs, configurable via `-avalancheminquorumnodes` for tests), **AND**
     - `avaproofsNodeCounter >= minAvaproofsNodeCount` (minimum peers that have completed `avaproofs` synchronization outside IBD).
  2. **Minimum Relative Staked Coins (`minQuorumStakeRatio` of Live `MonetaryState.current_supply`):**
     - Let `current_supply_atoms` be the active tip's `MonetaryState.current_supply` (`DiskStateStore::ReadTipMonetaryState()`).
     - Require total known finalized proof stake `totalPeersStakeAtoms` to satisfy:
       $$\text{totalPeersStakeAtoms} \ge \max\left(\text{minQuorumStakeAtoms},\; \lfloor \text{current\_supply\_atoms} \times \text{minQuorumSupplyRatio} \rfloor\right)$$
       (where `minQuorumSupplyRatio` defaults to e.g. `1%` of circulating supply, configurable via `-avalancheminquorumsupplyratio`, and `minQuorumScore` remains configurable via `-avalancheminquorumscore`).
     - During Genesis cold-start (before coinbase maturity or before stakers register proofs), `totalPeersStakeAtoms == 0`, so this check is automatically `false` with zero height special-casing!
  3. **Minimum Connected Stake Ratio (`minQuorumConnectedScoreRatio`):**
     - `connectedPeersScore >= std::round(double(totalPeersScore) * minQuorumConnectedScoreRatio)` (default `0.80`, ensuring at least 80% of the registered stake weight is actively online and reachable).
- **Graceful Tier-0 Behavior When `!isQuorumEstablished()`:**
  - `isPreconsensusActivated(pindexPrev)`, `isStakingRewardsActivated(pindexPrev)`, and `isTreasuryGovernanceActivated(pindexPrev)` all check `m_avalanche && m_avalanche->isQuorumEstablished()`.
  - While `!isQuorumEstablished()`:
    - `BuildV2CoinbaseTx` (`src/node/miner.cpp`) allocates **100% of net claimable block reward** (`S_n + fees - burn`) to `vtx[0].vout[0]` (the miner's `scriptPubKeyIn`), and `ConnectTip` (`src/validation.cpp`) connects valid PoW blocks directly in Tier 0 without parking.
  - As soon as enough Avalanche nodes and relative staked coins come online (`isQuorumEstablished() == true`) and finalize winners for `pindexPrev->GetBlockHash()`:
    - `BuildV2CoinbaseTx` includes the `25%` Staker output (`getStakingRewardWinners`) and `25%` Treasury output (`getTreasuryWinner`), and `PreConsensusPolicy`, `StakingRewardsPolicy`, and `TreasuryPolicy` enforce soft block-parking on new tips.
